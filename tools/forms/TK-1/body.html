What to Print, and in What Order

Duplex. The backs are the reverse of the fronts, so print the duplex order on a printer set to two-sided, flip on the long edge (landscape sheets, bound on the short edge; some drivers call it “long-edge binding”). Test one sheet first: the front’s tab is on the right and its back’s tab comes out on the left of the same sheet, at the same height. If the back comes out upside down, the driver is flipping on the short edge.

Size. The book page is 8.82 by 5.82 in, the trimmed size of the assessor’s bound books (every measurement comes from the Illustrator files). It prints centred on Letter landscape with trim marks at the four corners: cut 1.09 in off the left and the right and 1.34 in off the top and the bottom of every sheet (a Board with six to ten tokens is taller, 7.46 in: cut 0.52 in top and bottom). The 11 in wide page is the same page enlarged to the full width: cut 0.62 in off the top and the bottom. “Fill the Letter page” needs no cutting: only the white space between the elements grows. Print at 100%, not “fit to page”: the cards are cut to sit exactly in the boxes. The binding margin is the panel’s own inset on the bound side. Card sheets are full Letter.

Long backs. The text of a back is set at the files’ 12.3 pt (15.3 pt on the 11 in page) and never below 11 pt, with 3 percent kept in hand so a page that fits on screen also fits on paper. On the Token Economy back the credit line gives way first: it drops to one small line at the foot. What still does not fit continues on a second back page (in a duplex order a blank sheet is added first so every back stays on the reverse of its front).

Spares. The sheet of one card prints a portrait Letter page of that card repeated with cut lines, 5 across at about 1.5 in or 6 across at about 1.25 in: the smaller sizes are the samples’ own; a Letter sheet holds 6 or 7 rows of them.

What This Form Makes

A token board book: four laminated landscape pages bound along the left edge, each with a coloured tab on the right so the four show when the book is closed. The Choices page (“What Are You Earning?”) holds six picture cards of the things the student likes; the Targets page (“First: Teaching Targets”) holds six cards of the skills or replacement behaviors being taught; the Board carries the student’s photo, a First box and a Then box, and a strip of token slots; the Tokens page is where the token cards wait. The back of each page carries its instructions. The adult puts the chosen target card under First and the chosen reward card under Then; the student earns tokens one at a time into the slots and, when the strip is full, gets what is under Then.

The arrangement is the Premack principle made visible (Premack, 1959): access to the more probable, preferred activity is made contingent on the less preferred one, which is what “First, then” says. The tokens are a token reinforcement system (Hackenberg, 2018): the token is a conditioned reinforcer earned on a token-production schedule and exchanged, when the strip is full, for the backup reinforcer under Then. Form TE-1 designs the schedules (how much work earns a token, how many tokens earn the exchange) and Form VS-1 prints the simpler strips; this form prints the book itself.

Making the Book

1. Fill the pagesSetup: the student, the first name as it prints, the photo, the token count and picture, the colours and the QR link. Choices and Targets: six pictures each, from the library or the student’s own photos (upload them through the picker). Choose all six pictures at once opens the picker on Several at once: tap the pictures in order (each shows its number), then Put them on the cards; Choose on one row changes that card, and the arrows move a card up or down. The two photos on the Board face each other: the right one is mirrored, as in the samples, or the left one, or neither (Setup). Board: leave First and Then empty for the cards, or set a fixed picture. Backs: the text is the assessor’s and can be edited. The simulator on the toolbar fills a sample student so the book can be seen at once.
2. PrintChoose the order on Setup. Fronts only gives the four pages; fronts and backs interleaves them for a duplex printer (two-sided, flip on the long edge; test one sheet); card sheets prints the choices, the targets and the tokens with cut lines; all prints everything; a sheet of one card prints spares. Print at 100%. From a computer every page is landscape Letter except the spare sheet, which is portrait. From an iPad or iPhone, Safari prints only portrait, so each book page is printed turned on its side on a portrait sheet at full size, and the card sheets are laid out portrait (cards at the 11 in size can take a second sheet); Setup’s Sheets can force either way. In each case the book page (8.82 by 5.82 in, or 11 in wide) is centred with trim marks, and Setup says how much to cut off.
3. Laminate and cutLaminate the four pages and the card sheets (5 mil or thicker pouches hold up). Cut the cards on the cut lines, rounding the corners a little so they do not catch.
4. Hook-and-loop dotsPut the hook side on every circle guide (the six boxes on Choices and on Targets, the First and Then boxes, the token slots, the token park boxes) and the loop side on the back of every card and token, so the cards do not catch on clothing.
5. BindPunch and bind along the left edge (spiral or comb) in the order Choices, Targets, Board, Tokens; the backs fall in place. The binding margin is already on the pages.
6. Teach itMake the tokens worth something first, as the Token Economy back says, and keep at it until the student reaches for the token: give a token and exchange it at once for the backup reinforcer (sampling); prompt the target response, give the token and exchange it at once (coaching); give the token right after the target response, exchange it at once, then begin to ask for more tokens before each exchange (conditioning). The check that it worked is that the behavior earning tokens goes up. Decide the schedule before starting (the “Not So FAST” paragraph on the Token Board back): a token after each response for a new skill, raised a little at a time as the skill holds, and back one step if the behavior falls apart (ratio strain). Keep the item under Then out of reach at other times, or the board loses its pull. The schedules are written on Form TE-1.

In the Assessor’s Words

From the backs: a token economy “is a program in which tokens are earned for specific behaviors and later exchanged for things the learner wants”; tokens “bridge the gap between the moment the behavior happens and the moment the real reinforcer is delivered, which helps teach waiting”; and before starting, “decide how much of the behavior earns one token: how many responses, or how long”, because “this is the schedule of reinforcement, and it must not ask too much”. The backs’ text was checked against the published literature in v21.42b and revised only where it was inaccurate or incomplete; the assessor’s structure, headings and voice are kept, and every back still fits its page.

Sources for the Backs

Premack (1959) for the principle; Timberlake and Allison (1974) for the condition that the preferred activity be restricted (response deprivation); Hackenberg (2018) for token reinforcement, token conditioning and exchange schedules; Ivy, Meindl, Overley and Robson (2017) for the components a token economy should specify; Russell, Ingvarsson, Haggar and Jessel (2018) for tokens as generalized conditioned reinforcers that hold up when one backup item has lost its value; Tiger, Hanley and Hernandez (2006) and Dyer, Dunlap and Winterling (1990) for choice (preferred by learners, and lowering problem behavior), with Lerman and colleagues (1997) for its limit (choice among items already highly preferred did not raise responding); Horner and Day (1991) for a replacement behavior that is easier than the problem behavior; Cooper, Heron and Heward (2020) for schedule thinning and ratio strain.
