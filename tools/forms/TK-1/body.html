What to Print, and in What Order

Duplex. The backs are the reverse of the fronts, so print the duplex order on a printer set to two-sided, flip on the long edge (landscape sheets, bound on the short edge; some drivers call it “long-edge binding”). Test one sheet first: the front’s tab is on the right and its back’s tab comes out on the left of the same sheet, at the same height. If the back comes out upside down, the driver is flipping on the short edge.

Size. The book page is 11 by 7.33 in, the size of the samples and the bound books. It prints centred on Letter landscape with trim marks at the four corners: cut 0.58 in off the top and the bottom of every sheet. Tick “Fill the Letter page” to keep the full 8.5 in height instead (only the white space between the elements grows). Print at 100%, not “fit to page”: the cards are cut to sit exactly in the boxes. The page keeps a 0.45 in binding margin on the left of every front and the right of every back. Card sheets are full Letter.

Long backs. The text of a back is set at 16 pt and never below 15 pt; what does not fit continues on a second back page (in a duplex order a blank sheet is added first so every back stays on the reverse of its front).

Spares. The sheet of one card prints a portrait Letter page of that card repeated with cut lines, 5 across at about 1.5 in or 6 across at about 1.25 in: the smaller sizes are the samples’ own; a Letter sheet holds 6 or 7 rows of them.

The Board

The student’s photo in both top corners (one, at the top right, on the Rules-row layout), “First” over a grey box and “Then” over a green box, and the token slots along the bottom. Leave the First and Then boxes empty for the cards, or choose a picture to print in a box as a fixed preset (the picture then prints as a card inside the box).

The Instruction Backs

The text on the back of each page, in the assessor’s words. Markup: **bold**, *italic*, __underlined__, ## a centred heading; a blank line starts a paragraph; {n} prints the token count in words and {token}, {tokens} and {TOKENS} the token’s name. Long text is set smaller to fit the page.

How to use: the insert

Three steps in the assessor’s words, printed as a two-page insert after the backs when ticked on Setup (Steps 1 and 2 on the first page, Step 3 on the second). The first line of each block is its heading.

Preview: the Book as It Prints

What This Form Makes

A token board book: four laminated landscape pages bound along the left edge, each with a coloured tab on the right so the four show when the book is closed. The Choices page (“What Are You Earning?”) holds six picture cards of the things the student likes; the Targets page (“First: Teaching Targets”) holds six cards of the skills or replacement behaviors being taught; the Board carries the student’s photo, a First box and a Then box, and a strip of token slots; the Tokens page is where the token cards wait. The back of each page carries its instructions. The adult puts the chosen target card under First and the chosen reward card under Then; the student earns tokens one at a time into the slots and, when the strip is full, gets what is under Then.

The arrangement is the Premack principle made visible (Premack, 1959): access to the more probable, preferred activity is made contingent on the less preferred one, which is what “First, then” says. The tokens are a token reinforcement system (Hackenberg, 2018): the token is a conditioned reinforcer earned on a token-production schedule and exchanged, when the strip is full, for the backup reinforcer under Then. Form TE-1 designs the schedules (how much work earns a token, how many tokens earn the exchange) and Form VS-1 prints the simpler strips; this form prints the book itself.

Making the Book

1. Fill the pagesSetup: the student, the first name as it prints, the photo, the token count and picture, the colours and the QR link. Choices and Targets: six pictures each, from the library or the student’s own photos (upload one through the picker). Board: leave First and Then empty for the cards, or set a fixed picture. Backs: the text is the assessor’s and can be edited. The simulator on the toolbar fills a sample student so the book can be seen at once.
2. PrintChoose the order on Setup. Fronts only gives the four pages; fronts and backs interleaves them for a duplex printer (two-sided, flip on the long edge; test one sheet); card sheets prints the choices, the targets and the tokens with cut lines; all prints everything; a sheet of one card prints spares. Print at 100%. Every page is landscape Letter except the spare sheet, which is portrait.
3. Laminate and cutLaminate the four pages and the card sheets (5 mil or thicker pouches hold up). Cut the cards on the dashed lines, rounding the corners a little so they do not catch.
4. Hook-and-loop dotsPut the hook side on every circle guide (the six boxes on Choices and on Targets, the First and Then boxes, the token slots, the token park boxes) and the loop side on the back of every card and token, so the cards do not catch on clothing.
5. BindPunch and bind along the left edge (spiral or comb) in the order Choices, Targets, Board, Tokens; the backs fall in place. The binding margin is already on the pages.
6. Teach itCondition the tokens first, as the Token Economy back says: deliver a token and at once the backup reinforcer (sampling), prompt a response and deliver the token (coaching), then deliver the token right after the target response (conditioning). Decide the schedule before starting (the “Not So FAST” paragraph on the Token Board back): a token after each response for a new skill, thinned as the skill holds. The schedules are written on Form TE-1.

In the Assessor’s Words

From the backs: a token economy “is a behavior-based program in which tokens are earned contingent on certain behaviors”; token economies “help teach ‘waiting’ and they can help bridge the gap between when desired behaviors occur and when reinforcement is provided”; and before starting, “decide how long you would like them to display the behavior or how many responses you would like to see before they receive a token”, because “it is essential to ensure that you are not requiring too much and setting the bar too high for your learner”.
