What This Form Keeps

A consultation note is the record that a service happened and what it consisted of. It has to serve three readers at once: the BCBA a month later, who needs to know what was recommended and whether anyone did it; the team, who need the changes to the plan and the authority behind them; and anyone who later audits the service, who needs the date, the time, the people, the setting and the signature. The note on this form is arranged in the order of a visit (who and where, what was looked at, what was found, what was advised, what was changed, what was taught, what comes next) so that writing it is a matter of filling the rows, and the log makes every note findable.

Two things on the note are computed rather than written. The duration comes from the start and end times, and the time inside the visit by category (observation, consultation, training, direct service, meeting) is compared with it; documentation and travel are outside the visit and are added up on their own. The week’s figure is compared with the goal level in the direction the goal runs, so the note says in one line where the week stands; whether the goal is met is a run of consecutive measurements, which the note takes from Form DD-1 and never judges from one week. The integrity score links the note to the Form TI-1 observation it rests on, read in TI-1’s own bands, since a recommendation to change a plan that is not being run is a different recommendation from one about a plan that is.

What the Research and the Standards Support

Consultation is a problem-solving sequence with data at each stageBergan and Kratochwill (1990) describe behavioral consultation as four stages (problem identification, problem analysis, plan implementation, plan evaluation), three of them carried by a structured interview (problem identification, problem analysis, plan evaluation); plan implementation is monitored between them, and the decisions are made on data. The note’s order follows that sequence: what the data show, what the problem is, what the plan change is, what is evaluated next. Erchul and Martens (2010) review the evidence for the model in schools and the conditions under which consultee behavior changes; the training and next-steps rows are where those conditions are recorded.
The family belongs in the sequenceConjoint behavioral consultation (Sheridan, Kratochwill & Bergan, 1996) runs the same four stages with the parent and the teacher together. A parent meeting on this form is a note of the same shape as a school consultation, so a home decision and a school decision sit in one log.
Recommendations without follow-up are not implementedNoell et al. (2005) followed the plans written for 45 elementary students referred for consultation, each with their teacher, and compared weekly follow-up meetings, a commitment emphasis, and performance feedback on the teacher’s own implementation data. Performance feedback produced better implementation and better student outcomes than the other two. A stored integrity score is not feedback given: the “feedback to the implementer” row records that the implementer was shown their own data (which TI-1, which graph) and what they will change, and with the next steps (an owner and a date) and the open-steps table on the Log it is the note’s share of that loop. A note that records a recommendation without a step that has an owner and a date says so in its check.
The Ethics Code requires the recordThe Behavior Analyst Certification Board’s (2020) Ethics Code for Behavior Analysts requires behavior analysts to create and maintain documentation of their professional work, to protect and retain it, to report their services accurately, and to collect data and evaluate interventions continually (sections 2.05, 2.06, 2.17 and 2.18 in the Code’s numbering). The date, times, setting, people, signature and time-by-category rows exist so that each note satisfies those provisions on its face, and the log keeps the retention record.
Decisions are made on the data, in the direction of the goalCooper, Heron and Heward (2020) set out data-based decision making: a measure compared with a criterion, a trend read before a change, and the change recorded with its date so the graph can show what it did. The rate-against-goal line and the plan-in-force row are the note’s share of that record; the graphs themselves live on the data forms the note cites.

How the Figures Are Calculated

DurationEnd time minus start time, in minutes. Times are read as h:mm with an optional am or pm; a time without am or pm is read on the 24-hour clock, and an end time earlier than the start is taken as the afternoon of the same day.
Time by categoryThe five categories inside the visit (observation, consultation, training, direct service, meeting) are summed and compared with the duration. A sum within 5 minutes of the duration is accepted; otherwise the note says by how much the two differ (a working convention). Documentation and travel are outside the start-to-end span: they are shown as minutes outside the visit, with their own total, and are not compared. The categories are optional; a BCBA who does not bill by the hour can leave them empty.
Rate against goalOne week’s figure against the goal level: for a behavior to reduce, at or below the level; for a skill to build, at or above it. “Close” means within 25% of the level on the wrong side (a working convention). This is a level for the week, not the goal: the goal names a number of consecutive measurements, so the note gives Form DD-1’s run of consecutive recorded days at the aim against the number its aim asks for, never “goal met” from one week, and asks for the goal’s own criterion (Form GB-1) to be checked before the goal is called met. From the case, the week’s figure is DD-1’s mean for the week shown on its week summary, and the phase average is given on its own line.
IntegrityThe TI-1 score is placed in Form TI-1’s own bands and words, for the plan type set on the note: 90% and above, acceptable with routine monitoring; 80% to 89%, acceptable for a behavior-reduction plan with a re-check scheduled, and a caution for a skill-acquisition program; 60% to 79%, a caution (retrain any consequence step in error); below 60%, not acceptable: the student’s data are not read as a test of the plan. These are TI-1’s working conventions (see its Guide). When the critical-step figure is below TI-1’s criterion, the note says to treat the plan as not yet running as written, as TI-1 does. Who observed, and whether at this visit or reported, are written beside the score.
Hours by monthEach note’s duration, in hours to two decimals, summed by the month of its date and by its type. Notes without a readable date are listed under “undated”.
Open next stepsSteps not ticked done, from every note; a step is overdue when its date is before today.
Service log entries (v21.85)Each dated note with minutes is one entry; it is certified and counted once the note is signed, and listed as not yet signed until then. A scheduled session not held is listed on its own with the reason, the minutes scheduled and the make-up date; it delivers no minutes. The missed minutes are added up by reason (student reasons: absent, not available; provider reasons: provider absent; and staff not available, school closed, other), with the minutes made up; whether a missed session is owed is the district’s rule. Its minutes are the service minutes typed on the note, else the duration from the start and end times; its kind is the one chosen, else the type’s (an observation is observation, a consultation or a call is consultation, a training is training, a direct session is direct service, a parent or IEP meeting is a meeting, a record review is other), else the category with the most minutes; with whom is the one typed, else the roles named in brackets under who was present (a parent meeting is with the parent, an IEP meeting with the team, a direct session with the student); the one line is the one typed, else the purpose. A note without a date, or without minutes or times, is not an entry.
This week, this month, the IEPThe week runs Monday to Sunday and the month is the calendar month, both the ones containing today. Against the IEP’s minutes (Form DM-1, per week, month or grading period, between the dates typed for the grading period), the minutes counted are those of the kind the IEP names, from signed notes: for consultation, the consultation, observation and training notes (a call with the team is consultation); for direct, direct service; for both, all of those. IEP and team meetings, parent and consent meetings, record reviews and the assessment are not counted by default and are shown on their own line; a note’s “Counts toward the IEP service” box settles an edge case (parent training the IEP lists, for one). The district’s own service-log rules decide the edge cases. “120 of 120 minutes this month” is the minutes counted against the minutes owed.

What Makes a Note Hold Up

Written the same dayA note written from memory a week later is weaker as a record and less useful as a plan. The “note written on” row is there so a late note says it is late, and a note signed after the date of the contact says “late entry”.
Signed once it is writtenThe signature is the BCBA’s statement that the note is accurate, so a new note starts unsigned. Sign this note writes the name and credential from the case with the date and time, and only once the note says what was done and why. A signed note is locked; a later correction or addition goes in as a dated addendum, and a signature withdrawn to correct the note stays on the record as withdrawn (BACB Ethics Code 2.06, accuracy in reporting).
Observation separated from judgment“What was seen” is what happened, in order; “Problems identified” is what it means. A reader should be able to disagree with the second while accepting the first.
Changes name their authorityA change made under a decision rule the plan already writes (on Form TD-1, the thinning rule: advance or step back one level; the integrity rule) is the BCBA’s call, and the note names the rule. Any other change is a revision of the plan: the IEP team’s decision, at a meeting or by a written amendment the parent agrees to without one (34 CFR 300.324(a)(4)), with prior written notice when it changes the provision of FAPE (34 CFR 300.503(a)), and with the parent’s informed consent when it is substantial (BACB Ethics Code 2.11), recorded as its own event on Form IC-1’s consent record. The consent on Form IC-1 covers the assessment and the writing of the plan, not carrying it out or changing it. A change the BCBA only recommends is “recommended only; awaiting the team”. The note records which, with the dates; it does not change the plan: a revision goes on Form TD-1 and its phase line on Form DD-1, and the staff handouts (Forms QS-1 and EB-1) follow.
Every recommendation has a next stepWhen recommendations are given, at least one next step has an owner and a date; the note’s check asks for it. A change recommended but not yet decided is recorded as “recommended only; awaiting the team”, with the step that takes it to the team, and reappears on the open-steps table until it is either adopted or dropped.
Safety at every visit“Safety since the last visit” is filled from the case: the restraints this semester (Form CR-1), the incidents since the last note and whether home was told (Form DD-1), and the latest injury record (Form IM-1). A second restraint in a semester is flagged where the note is written: s. 1003.573(6)(a), F.S., as Form CR-1 quotes it, requires a crisis intervention plan. The line does not replace the incident report or the notice to the parent.
Assent on a direct sessionA direct session records whether the student gave assent and how they showed it, any withdrawal and how it was honored, and how they took part, with Form SI-1’s assent plan shown from the case (BACB Ethics Code 2.11: obtain assent from clients when applicable).
From the first contactThe consent meeting, the assessment interviews and the FBA observations are notes too, so the hours and the service log cover the whole case and the FBA’s own dates can be read off the log; the plan rows stay out of the way on those notes. They are not counted toward the IEP’s service minutes unless set to.
A session not held is recordedA scheduled session that did not happen is a note of the type “Scheduled session not held”, with the reason, the minutes scheduled and the make-up date, so the service log shows why minutes are short and whether they were made up (34 CFR 300.323(c)(2): services made available in accordance with the IEP).
The note cites its dataWhich forms, which dates. The note does not repeat the graph; it says where the graph is.
The service log stands on the notesThe printed service log is a summary of the notes, not a substitute for them: each certified line points to a dated, signed note that says what the service consisted of, and prints its date signed; a note not yet signed is listed below the certification, not above it. Where the district bills school-based Medicaid, it sets which fields its log needs (commonly the student and Medicaid ID, the date, the start and end times or the minutes, the service, the provider’s name and credential, and a signature); check the printed page against the district’s own requirements before using it as the billing record.
Nothing about third parties that is not neededOther students are not named; a peer is “a peer”. Staff are named with their role because the plan assigns them steps.

Writing help

A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print.

About the simulation

Load simulation (toolbar) replaces every entry with a worked example; nothing in it is real data. The simulated case is seven notes over five weeks (two observations with TI-1 scores, a phone call, a parent meeting, an IEP meeting that adopts a revision with its notice and the parent’s consent, a scheduled consultation not held because the teacher was called away, and the consultation that made it up), each signed, with next steps, time by category and the hours log; the service log counts them against an IEP of 120 minutes a month of consultation, leaving the parent and IEP meetings out.
