The Supervisee

Entered once for the year; every month’s record carries it. Supervising more than one RBT? Use Add an RBT at the top: each RBT has their own record in this file, and the supervisors and rules below are entered once for all of them. Use client codes, not student names: this record may be shown to the BACB in an audit.

Supervisors

An RBT Supervisor is a BCBA or a BCaBA; an RBT Requirements Coordinator is a BCBA and may also supervise. Before supervising, each completes training based on the Supervisor Training Curriculum Outline (2.0), uploaded to their BACB portal. With a Requirements Coordinator, only the coordinator lists the RBT in the portal. The RBT may not be related to, superior to, an employer of, or in a multiple relationship with a supervisor or the coordinator, and every client the RBT works with needs a supervisor with client oversight (employed by the same organization as the RBT, or under contract with the client).

Supervision Contacts

Each real-time contact with the supervisor: in person or by live two-way video. In person is preferred; a web camera or video conference is allowed. A phone call, an email, a recording, or video monitoring without real-time interaction and feedback can be noted here but does not count. Supervision is client-focused and is not professional development: one event cannot count as both.

The Credential

2026 recertification with the Recertification Competency Assessment

An RBT who recertifies in 2026 does so one last time under the old requirements: a Recertification Competency Assessment, completed no more than 45 days before the expiration date and overseen by a qualified BACB-certified RBT supervisor, uploaded with the recertification application. The two-year cycle begins after it. Check against the current BACB RBT Handbook.

The two-year cycle and its PDUs

Professional development units count only inside the cycle: they cannot be made up in the reinstatement period, extra PDUs do not carry over, and repeating the same activity does not count again. Three kinds count: an in-service training at the RBT’s organization related to behavior-analytic service delivery, given by a BCaBA, BCBA or BCBA-D (not training on one client’s program; documented on the RBT Professional Development form); an ACE Provider event; a didactic behavior-analytic university course (1 semester credit = 15 hours, 1 quarter credit = 10). Ongoing supervision, client-specific training and supervised fieldwork never count. Check against the current BACB RBT Handbook.

Self-reporting. An RBT self-reports certain events to the BACB within 30 days of becoming aware of them (RBT Ethics Code 2.0 and the self-reporting requirements), reports noncompliance with the supervision requirements in a timely manner, and updates personal information within 30 days of a change. This is a reminder, not legal advice: the RBT and the supervisor read the current requirements themselves.

Concerns and Corrective Action (confidential)

When the supervisor becomes aware of a potential ethics violation by the RBT, they investigate and document it. A Notice of Alleged Violation goes to the BACB when the concern involves abuse or neglect of a client; results in the RBT being charged with or convicted of a violation of the law; violates RBT Ethics Code (2.0) standard 1.03, 1.05, 3.01, 3.02, 3.03, 3.04 or 3.05; or has occurred repeatedly despite reasonable attempts to address it. Otherwise the supervisor develops and carries out an individualized corrective-action plan and documents the outcome (BCBA Handbook). This part is not on the monthly record; it prints only from its own button.

What This Form Keeps

The record of an RBT’s ongoing supervision, month by month: the days and hours the RBT provided behavior-analytic services, and each real-time contact with a supervisor (when, how long, with whom, individual or in a small group, in person or by live video, whether the supervisor watched the RBT working with a client, what was covered, how it was taught, the feedback, and what the RBT works on next). Each month is checked against the rules on the Setup page as it is filled, so a gap shows while there is still time in the month to close it. The signed monthly record is the document the BACB can ask for in an audit.

How the Figures Are Calculated

RBT hoursEach service day: end time less start time, less the minutes not in RBT services. A day ticked “No service” counts nothing. In the current month only the days up to today count; later rows are shown as planned.
Supervision hoursThe minutes of the contacts that count: in person or by live video (web camera or video conference), with a supervisor who is a BCBA or a BCaBA on the Setup page, on a date inside the month, individual or in a group of no more than 10 RBTs. A contact by phone, email or recording, video monitoring without real-time interaction, a group of more than 10, or a supervisor without one of those credentials is listed but not counted, and the month is flagged.
Supervisor requirements on recordFor each supervisor with a contact in the month: supervisor training (Supervisor Training Curriculum Outline 2.0) marked done, the relationship check confirmed, client oversight recorded, and the RBT listed in the BACB portal (by the Requirements Coordinator when there is one, otherwise by the supervisor).
A month without servicesTicked “No services this month”, a month is not applicable: an RBT who does not practise in a month needs no supervision that month.
Percent supervisedSupervision minutes divided by RBT service minutes. “More minutes needed” is the rule’s percent of the RBT minutes so far, rounded up, less the supervision minutes so far; the planned figure uses every row of the month.
Contacts, individual, observationCounted from the contacts that count. A contact is individual when only this RBT took part, and a small-group contact otherwise; an observation is a contact in which the supervisor watched the RBT providing services with a client.
The month’s statusGreen: met. Amber: not yet (the month has not ended). Red: not met after the month ended, or a contact that cannot count (phone, email, recording, video monitoring alone, a group of more than 10, a supervisor without a BCBA or BCaBA credential) at any time. Grey: not applicable (no services).
Observation checklistItems marked Yes divided by the items marked Yes or No; N/A is left out. The year view averages the month’s checklists (a working convention: 90% and above is solid, 80% to 89% is a coaching point, below 80% calls for retraining with behavior skills training).
The credentialDays until the expiration date on the Setup page, with an early reminder and a reminder from the opening of the competency assessment window (the days on the Rules panel). PDUs are totalled from the log against the cycle’s target: only the three kinds that count, dated inside the cycle; an entry that does not count is marked.

Good Practice

Several RBTsOne file can hold every RBT you supervise. Add an RBT at the top of the page starts a new record (the school year and the usual days come from the RBT on screen); the Supervisee list and the table under it show each RBT with the month on screen, and choosing one shows that RBT’s setup, months, goals, credential and concerns. The supervisors and the rules are entered once and shared. Each RBT’s monthly record prints and is signed on its own; the CSV holds every RBT’s rows.
Write it the same dayA contact recorded the day it happened, with the feedback in the supervisor’s own words, is a better record and a better plan for the next one.
Behavior skills trainingInstruction, modeling, rehearsal and feedback, to a criterion, is the method with the strongest evidence for teaching staff (Parsons, Rollyson & Reid, 2012). Tick the parts used, so the record shows how a skill was taught as well as what.
FeedbackSpecific, prompt and tied to what was observed: what went well, one thing to change, and how (Reid, Parsons & Green, 2012). The follow-up box carries it into the next contact.
Codes, not namesClients are named by a code. The record goes to the BACB in an audit and should identify the RBT and the supervisors, not the students.
Keep it 7 yearsKeep the signed monthly records (and this file) for at least 7 years, also after the supervision ends. The BACB lists what they must show: the days and times the RBT provided services; the dates and duration of supervision; its format (one-on-one or group); the dates of direct observations with a client; the names of the supervisors; proof of the supervisor’s relationship to the client; and other records (such as session notes) where records disagree. The monthly record prints all of these. In an audit the BACB typically allows 14 days to submit them.

Writing help

A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print.
