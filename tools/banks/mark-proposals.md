# Marks proposed for 254 steps of Form TD-1's bank

**Applied in v26.11.** The BCBA approved the 228 proposed marks on 11/13 ("Yes I approve."): sections 1 to 4 below are now in the bank as proposed. **The 26 questions of section 5 are still open**: none of those steps was changed, and each needs an answer by its number (extinction, restrictive, or no mark). The record behind this page is `mark-proposals.json`; each applied item carries the version.

## Why this page

A step that starts "EXTINCTION:" or "RESTRICTIVE:" is known to Form TD-1 by that mark: an extinction step shows "not yet agreed" until extinction is agreed on the Responding to behavior sheet, and a restrictive step needs the district's review and written consent. When the extinction mark came in (v25.83) only the steps that used the word were read. Steps that describe extinction in other words ("give no attention after", "do not hand over the item", "keep the task going") were left for a later reading. This is that reading.

## What was read

- Every unmarked step of the 3,290 cards was searched for words of withholding (ignore, withhold, give no, do not give or respond, no attention, without comment, keep the task in place, and the like) together with a word for a problem behavior, and for words of blocking or holding. That found 430 steps. Each was read against the reader rules (rules 3, 3a and 4).
- **254 are proposed below. 176 were read and need no mark**: a signal-off part of a multiple or chained schedule, a reset of a DRO interval, behavior that does not depend on others (ignoring it withholds nothing), an assessment or test condition, a step that only refers to the plan, a step that says the study used no extinction, an ordinary accepted prompt, a hand held for safety on a street.
- **A limit.** The search was by words. A step that withholds something without naming the behavior in the same sentence was not found by it; about 210 such steps remain unread, and most are teaching steps ("give no praise in the test"). Say so if you want those read too.

## What approving changes

- **Extinction (152 steps):** the step gets "EXTINCTION:" in front. On those cards TD-1 then shows "Extinction step: not yet agreed" until you agree extinction for that plan, and the printed plan carries the note.
- **Restrictive (76 steps):** the step gets "RESTRICTIVE:" in front and the sentence that it needs district review and written consent. 66 are escape extinction (the task stays, no break follows the behavior), 3 guide the student through a demand, 7 block a behavior or hold the hands inside teaching. For escape extinction TD-1 also points to its consent block.
- **Questions (26 steps):** I could not decide from the card. Each says why.

## How to answer

Any of these is enough: "approve all"; "approve the extinction list"; "approve all but 17, 44 and 203"; or an answer for each question by its number (extinction, restrictive, or no mark).

## 1. Extinction (152)

Each step withholds attention, an item or an activity after the problem behavior. Proposed mark: EXTINCTION.

1. **Reward finished instructions with a break plus a preferred item, then thin the work requirement** (Piazza et al., 1997a; step 8 of 10; `dnra-break-item-thinning`)  
   Ignore any problem behavior that occurs during the break, and present the next instruction when the break ends.

2. **End the meal early when the student feeds themselves the offered bites or drinks** (Haney et al., 2023; step 7 of 9; `dnra-meal-ends-self-feed`)  
   Give no coaxing, reprimands or other reaction to pushing away, turning away, covering the mouth, crying, coughing or gagging.

3. **Brief on-topic conversation for appropriate talk, none for off-topic or sexual remarks** (Dixon et al., 2001; step 5 of 7; `dra-appropriate-talk-attention`)  
   Give no attention after an inappropriate statement and wait for the next appropriate one.

4. **Answer appropriate talk with brief attention and give none for disruptive talk** (Dwyer-Moore & Dixon, 2007; step 5 of 7; `dra-attention-appropriate-talk`)  
   Give no attention, comment or look of concern after disruptive vocalizations, and keep working.

5. **Reinforce following simple requests, which also lowered untreated problem behavior** (Parrish et al., 1986; step 6 of 7; `dra-compliance-praise`)  
   Give no reaction to aggression, disruption, property destruction or pica (no statements of concern), using only the least intrusive self-protection if the student hits.

6. **Cue-pause-point with praise for answering instead of echoing (attention-maintained immediate echolalia)** (Kaye et al., 2025; step 7 of 9; `dra-cpp-attention-echolalia`)  
   When the student repeats any part of your words, withhold all attention until the student has gone 3 s without echoing; end the session after 9 such trials to avoid evoking problem behavior.

7. **All-positive classroom plan: frequent praise, varied rewards and a daily reward menu** (Pfiffner et al., 1985; step 2 of 8; `dra-enhanced-positives`)  
   Stop reprimands and other negative consequences, and ignore minor misbehavior that is safe to ignore.

8. **Preferred toys and play for 30 s after each accepted bite, to treat food refusal** (Berth et al., 2019; step 7 of 8; `dra-feeding-play-after-bite`)  
   Give no toys or attention after head turning, pushing the spoon or covering the mouth; in the first phase, remove the bite for 30 s and then present the next bite.

9. **Praise for good table behavior and planned ignoring of disruption at meals** (Sajwaj & Hedges, 1973; step 3 of 5; `dra-mealtime-praise-ignore`)  
   Give no attention to disruption; if head banging or other unsafe behavior occurs, follow the student's safety plan instead of ignoring it.

10. **Parent praise for cooperative play and for following instructions, with planned ignoring of minor problem behavior** (Zeilberger et al., 1968; step 5 of 8; `dra-parent-attention-play`)  
   Ignore minor problem behavior: make no comment and do not turn to look when it happens.

11. **Teach a "please" sign, then answer only the sign instead of reaching, grabbing or leading** (Drasgow et al., 1998; step 8 of 10; `dra-please-sign-replace-reaching`)  
   When the student reaches, grabs, leads or yells, do not give the item; stay available and wait up to 30 s for the sign.

12. **Attention for an early signal that comes before self-injury, and no attention for the self-injury** (Dracobly & Smith, 2012; step 5 of 8; `dra-precursor-attention`)  
   Give no comment, reprimand or other new attention for self-injury; if it happens while attention is already under way, finish only the planned 5 to 10 s.

13. **Preferred-food bite and praise for accepting target foods at meals** (Riordan et al., 1984; step 7 of 10; `dra-preferred-bite-feeding`)  
   Ignore spitting out, crying and pushing the spoon away; give no attention or extra food for them.

14. **Preferred food or drink after each accepted bite (without escape extinction) for selective or low food intake** (Bachmeyer, 2009; step 5 of 8; `dra-preferred-food-bites`)  
   After a refusal or disruptive mealtime behavior, give no attention and no preferred item, and do not force the bite.

15. **Give the wanted toy when the student reaches for it, never after self-injury** (Vollmer et al., 1999a; step 5 of 8; `dra-reach-for-toys`)  
   When head hitting occurs, do not hand over a toy; wait for a reach.

16. **Red/green card game with points toward a class reward for attention-maintained classroom problem behavior** (B. J. Rodriguez et al., 2009; step 2 of 10; `dra-red-green-card`)  
   Tell the class the student is playing a game to earn a reward for everyone, and ask classmates to help by ignoring problem behavior.

17. **Red/green card game with points toward a class reward for attention-maintained classroom problem behavior** (B. J. Rodriguez et al., 2009; step 5 of 10; `dra-red-green-card`)  
   When problem behavior occurs, turn the card to red and do not talk to, look at, or reprimand the student.

18. **Give the item for a polite request, then build waiting up to 5 minutes before it comes** (Borrero & Vollmer, 2006; step 4 of 8; `dra-request-item-delay`)  
   Give no access to the item after aggression or disruption.

19. **Posted rules, praise for appropriate behavior and planned ignoring of minor disruption** (Madsen et al., 1968; step 3 of 8; `dra-rules-praise-ignoring`)  
   Ignore disruptive behavior that is safe to ignore (leaving the seat, calling out, noises, turning around): no reminders, scolding or warnings.

20. **Praise and a toy each time the student drinks from the cup within 8 seconds** (Peterson et al., 2015; step 8 of 10; `dra-self-drinking-toy`)  
   Give no reaction (no coaxing, reprimands or comments) to pushing, throwing or turning away, and do not replace a thrown or dumped cup until the next scheduled drink.

21. **Structured meal with praise and a toy for accepting bites (mild food selectivity)** (R. J. Clark et al., 2020; step 9 of 10; `dra-structured-meal`)  
   Withhold attention for coughing, gagging, crying and hitting during the meal, following the nurse's guidance on when gagging or vomiting needs a response.

22. **Teacher praise for task-relevant behavior with planned ignoring and a desk signal light** (M. H. Ward & Baker, 1968; step 4 of 7; `dra-teacher-praise-signal-light`)  
   Ignore disruptive behavior that is safe to ignore.

23. **Caution card: DRO still worked with some mixed errors but failed at 40% fidelity or less** (O’Neill et al., 2026; step 5 of 8; `dro-combined-fidelity-errors-caution`)  
   Give nothing right after the target behavior, even when it would end the behavior quickly.

24. **Resetting DRO with a chosen reward for staying close and walking during walks** (Roane & DeRosa, 2014; step 6 of 8; `dro-elopement-dropping-choice`)  
   After any elopement, dropping or attempt, restart the timer from the beginning, give no reprimands or statements of concern, and repeat "You need to walk with me."

25. **Praise after each 15 minutes free of head hitting and screaming, built up from short sessions to the whole day** (Clauser & Gould, 1988; step 5 of 8; `dro-praise-15min-generalize`)  
   When head hitting or screaming occurs, give no attention for it; if it becomes dangerous, follow the student's crisis plan.

26. **Whole-period quiet points in place of point fines for calling out and talking** (McLaughlin & Malaby, 1972b; step 3 of 6; `dro-quiet-period-points`)  
   During the period, ignore inappropriate verbalizations and do not take points away; keep a mental or written note of who spoke out.

27. **Resetting DRO: attention after each interval free of self-injury, lengthened from session data** (Vollmer et al., 1993; step 4 of 7; `dro-resetting-attention-intervals`)  
   When self-injury occurs, give no attention and restart the interval timer.

28. **DRO with attention and drinks for self-injury during free time** (Jensen & Heidorn, 1993; step 2 of 7; `dro-sib-freetime-attention`)  
   When self-injury or screaming occurs, do not talk to, touch or give a drink to the student at that moment, and start the period again.

29. **Variable-momentary DRO: check at unpredictable times and reinforce if the behavior is not happening** (Lindberg et al., 1999; step 6 of 9; `dro-variable-momentary`)  
   Give no attention or item after self-injury at any other time; let the interval run to its end.

30. **Variable-momentary DRO with attention for rare, intense aggression** (Kahng et al., 2001b; step 6 of 7; `dro-variable-momentary-attention`)  
   Give no attention after aggression at any other time, and follow the student's crisis plan to keep everyone safe.

31. **Whole-interval DRO to reduce behavior, then momentary DRO to maintain the reduction** (L. E. Barton et al., 1986; step 5 of 7; `dro-whole-then-momentary`)  
   Do not respond to the target behavior during the interval.

32. **DRO for attention-maintained self-injury, with attention always withheld after self-injury** (Mazaleski et al., 1993; step 4 of 8; `dro-with-attention-withheld`)  
   When self-injury occurs, give no attention of any kind (no concern, comments or redirection) and restart the 15-s timer.

33. **DRO for attention-maintained self-injury, with attention always withheld after self-injury** (Mazaleski et al., 1993; step 6 of 8; `dro-with-attention-withheld`)  
   Check that every adult near the student withholds attention after self-injury, because DRO without this step did not work even with 8 highly preferred items.

34. **Delay after a request, with the other preferred reinforcer offered during the wait** (Austin & Tiger, 2015; step 4 of 9; `dt_prog-alternative-reinforcer-wait`)  
   Do not answer a request that does not match the current situation, and give no reaction to aggression.

35. **Ask with a picture card, then wait for a larger amount while a timer runs** (Vollmer et al., 1999b; step 3 of 8; `dt_prog-signaled-wait-timer`)  
   Answer each request with the item and give no food or TV after aggression.

36. **After a request, have the student finish short tasks while waiting, adding tasks gradually** (Drifke et al., 2020; step 1 of 9; `dt_prog-task-during-delay`)  
   Teach the request first: remove the item or attention, prompt the request after 5 s, and give 30 s of the tablet or of play and talk for each request while problem behavior earns nothing.

37. **After a request, have the student finish short tasks while waiting, adding tasks gradually** (Drifke et al., 2020; step 6 of 9; `dt_prog-task-during-delay`)  
   Give no item or attention for problem behavior during the wait; keep prompting the task (tangible function) or simply wait (attention function).

38. **The waiting game in class: wait with other toys, a star token for each wait, three stars for a prize, waits lengthened to 5 min** (DeFreitas et al., 2024; step 6 of 7; `dt_prog-waiting-game-tokens-classroom`)  
   If self-injury or aggression occurs, end the trial, follow the safety plan and give no item; start again after 10 s calm.

39. **After a request, ask for a growing, varied number of tasks before the reward comes** (Iannaccone & Jessel, 2021; step 7 of 8; `dt_prog-work-before-reward`)  
   Do not give the item after problem behavior; wait for the request and the tasks.

40. **Room management for a group: one adult keeps everyone supplied with materials and prompts their use while a second works 5 min with each person in turn** (Cash et al., 1995; step 4 of 7; `ee-room-management-manager-and-individual-helper`)  
   Room manager: give no attention to inappropriate behavior, and leave alone whoever is working with the helper.

41. **Free access to toys or an enriched area, plus a matched procedure per function** (Smith et al., 1993; step 5 of 8; `ee-toys-multiply-controlled`)  
   Where self-injury persists with attention, give 5 s of attention after each interval without self-injury and ignore self-injury, starting at 15 s and lengthening to 30, 60 and then 120 s as it stays low.

42. **Choose the easiest request form so it wins out over aggression for toys** (Richman et al., 2001a; step 6 of 8; `effort-low-effort-mand`)  
   Withhold the toys after aggression and wait for a request before giving them again.

43. **Functional communication training to ask to be left alone or ask for toys, with scheduled free access** (Hagopian et al., 2001; step 5 of 8; `fct-alone-toys-ncr`)  
   Keep talking and commenting when problem behavior occurs, and do not give the preferred toy after problem behavior.

44. **Give the other functional reinforcer while the requested one is delayed after FCT** (Sumter et al., 2020; step 4 of 9; `fct-alt-reinforcer-during-delay`)  
   Give no reprimand, attention or tablet after problem behavior.

45. **Functional communication training with prompt delay for break or attention requests** (Kahng et al., 1997; step 8 of 8; `fct-arm-hi-finished-delay`)  
   Ignore self-injury maintained by attention and continue the activity.

46. **Ask for attention with a red/green availability board (attention-maintained vomiting, gagging and related behavior)** (Roth et al., 2025; step 5 of 9; `fct-attention-redgreen`)  
   Ignore every challenging behavior in the response class (vomiting, gagging, aggression, self-injury, disruption, verbal aggression and the rest) with no statements of concern; keep any clean-up brief and silent.

47. **Functional communication training for attention, with a student-choice check among approved packages** (Hanley et al., 2005; step 6 of 9; `fct-attention-treatment-choice`)  
   Give no reprimands or statements of concern after problem behavior, and keep working on your own task.

48. **FCT with a picture card for the type of attention the student prefers, chosen by a rapid attention-type assessment** (Strohmeier et al., 2018; step 6 of 9; `fct-attention-type-cards`)  
   Give no attention for problem behavior; from session 61 the study added 30 s of planned ignoring after each problem behavior, after which a card still on the green side could be exchanged.

49. **FCT for attention-maintained behavior using the kind of attention a quick parent-run assessment shows the student prefers** (Livingston et al., 2021; step 5 of 6; `fct-attention-type-from-rapid-assessment`)  
   Give no attention after problem behavior.

50. **Brief functional analysis followed by giving the maintaining consequence for a simple request** (Northup et al., 1991; step 6 of 8; `fct-brief-fa-please`)  
   When aggression is maintained by attention or a preferred item, ignore it (no consequence).

51. **Functional communication training to ask for attention only when the adult is free** (Kuhn et al., 2010; step 4 of 10; `fct-busy-free-adult-cues`)  
   Give no attention for problem behavior and carry on with the current activity.

52. **FCT with a card exchange for severe destructive behavior maintained by items: longer treatment alone does not prevent resurgence** (B. D. Greer et al., 2023; step 4 of 8; `fct-card-exchange-resurgence-duration`)  
   Do not give the items for destructive behavior; if a card exchange comes within 3 s after destructive behavior, wait for the next exchange that is free of destructive behavior before reinforcing.

53. **Card-exchange requests for items, with a written plan for lapses in answering them** (Helvey et al., 2023; step 9 of 10; `fct-card-lapse-plan`)  
   If a lapse happens, keep withholding the item after destructive behavior, resume answering the card as soon as possible, and log the lapse.

54. **Card-exchange FCT taught with a progressive prompt delay, then held on a lean variable-interval schedule for many sessions to limit relapse** (Fisher et al., 2018; step 6 of 10; `fct-card-lean-schedule`)  
   Give nothing for problem behavior, and if it occurs within 3 s of a card response, wait for a new card response with 3 s clear before honoring it.

55. **FCT in short trials: picture-card request for a toy or a break, taught by a parent coached by video** (T. N. Davis et al., 2023; step 3 of 7; `fct-card-parent-trials`)  
   Teaching trials: immediately prompt the student to touch the card; give the toy back, or remove the work, for the rest of the minute; give nothing for problem behavior.

56. **Thin a card request by taking the card out of reach for growing periods** (Roane et al., 2004; step 4 of 10; `fct-card-restriction-thinning`)  
   When aggression occurs, do not give the attention or the items for it, and continue the activity.

57. **Start functional communication training with a request card, then shift it step by step to a spoken request** (Randall et al., 2021; step 3 of 10; `fct-card-to-spoken-request`)  
   When problem behavior occurs, give nothing, wait 3 s with no problem behavior, then prompt the request.

58. **Functional communication training with competing items within reach while requests are thinned** (Hagopian et al., 2005; step 6 of 10; `fct-competing-items-thinning`)  
   Give no attention or item after problem behavior.

59. **Caution card: longer FCT alone does not prevent relapse when requests go unanswered** (B. D. Greer et al., 2023; step 4 of 9; `fct-duration-resurgence-caution`)  
   Withhold the item after destructive behavior, and if destructive behavior came right before a request, wait for the next request with none before answering.

60. **Functional communication training that honors every request when extinction is not always possible** (Worsdell et al., 2000; step 6 of 8; `fct-every-request-honored`)  
   Do not give the reinforcer after problem behavior whenever it can safely be withheld.

61. **Functional communication training that also gives other liked items with the requested reinforcer (combined reinforcers), with the student's preference checked** (Irwin Helvey et al., 2023; step 4 of 7; `fct-functional-plus-other-items`)  
   Give no reaction to problem behavior, and do not reinforce a request that comes within 5 s after problem behavior.

62. **Functional communication training: asking for a more suitable garment instead of self-injury** (Vollmer & Vorndran, 1998; step 5 of 8; `fct-garment-request-substitute`)  
   Do not give the item after self-injury, and stay calm and continue the session.

63. **Functional communication training: asking 'Am I doing good work?' to get adult attention** (Durand & Carr, 1992; step 7 of 9; `fct-good-work-attention`)  
   Ignore challenging behavior and keep the planned schedule of praise and directions going.

64. **Functional communication training with a picture-card touch that staff can guide, before a spoken request** (DeRosa et al., 2015a; step 7 of 9; `fct-guided-card-touch`)  
   For attention-maintained behavior, give no attention for problem behavior and stay with the other activity until the next trial.

65. **Three treatments matched to a brief assessment: timed attention plus a taught hand raise, a food request offered after every task trial, and leisure items when alone** (Sigafoos & Tucker, 2000; step 5 of 5; `fct-hand-raise-for-attention-and-food-requests-after-each-trial`)  
   Give no reaction to challenging behavior in any of the three.

66. **Functional communication training on a lean schedule, run longer, to limit relapse when requests go unanswered** (Fisher et al., 2018; step 5 of 9; `fct-lean-long-resurgence`)  
   Give nothing for problem behavior, and if it occurs within 3 s of a request, wait for a new request with 3 s free of problem behavior before giving the item or attention.

67. **Major problem behavior: extinction plus prompting an appropriate way to ask, and following through on demands** (King et al., 2020; step 2 of 6; `fct-major-behavior-extinction-prompt`)  
   When major problem behavior occurs, do not give the preferred item, activity or extra attention that it was getting.

68. **Pick the replacement request from the first appropriate response seen during extinction** (Grow et al., 2008; step 8 of 9; `fct-mand-from-extinction-variability`)  
   Give no programmed consequence for other responses or for problem behavior.

69. **Teach two new request forms, then let the student pick the one they prefer** (Winborn-Kemmerer et al., 2009; step 4 of 10; `fct-mand-preference-choice`)  
   Give 60 s of attention or of the item after each request, and give nothing for problem behavior while the reinforcer is unavailable.

70. **Finding the preferred way to ask by placing request forms on extinction one at a time within FCT** (Rocheleau & Livingston, 2026; step 2 of 9; `fct-modality-preference`)  
   Run 5-min FCT sessions with all three forms available, give 30 s with the preferred item after every request in any form, and do not give the item after problem behavior.

71. **FCT with several request modes taught, then the preferred mode placed on extinction in turn** (Livingston, Loder-Lafferty, et al., 2025; step 4 of 9; `fct-multimodal-sequential-ext`)  
   After challenging behavior, withhold the item and keep attention low; give no attention during the 30-s access, and do not honor a request that comes within 3 s of challenging behavior.

72. **Functional communication training, then thinning with signaled answer and wait periods** (Hanley et al., 2001; step 5 of 10; `fct-multiple-schedule-thinning`)  
   Give no reaction to problem behavior and continue the session.

73. **Parent-run FCT with a picture card at home after a coached brief functional analysis, for toddlers** (Gerow, Rivera, et al., 2020; step 5 of 7; `fct-parent-run-after-brief-fa`)  
   Give nothing for problem behavior, and wait until it has stopped for at least 3 s before prompting the card.

74. **Functional communication training: hand a picture card to get adult attention** (Thompson et al., 1998; step 6 of 8; `fct-picture-card-attention`)  
   Give no attention after hitting, kicking, pinching or scratching, and continue the session.

75. **Functional communication training: ask for play with a phrase or a card, with no attention for destructive behavior** (Hanley et al., 1997b; step 6 of 8; `fct-play-with-me-attention`)  
   Give no attention or other reaction to destructive behavior and keep the session going.

76. **Teach young students to ask "play with me" or "toys please" during classroom play** (Greer et al., 2013; step 6 of 8; `fct-play-with-me-preschool`)  
   Give no attention and no toys after aggression or property destruction.

77. **Function-based replacement behavior training in general education: teach and reinforce a replacement that gets the same outcome, run by the teacher** (McKenna et al., 2017; step 4 of 5; `fct-replacement-behavior-training-general-education`)  
   Honor the replacement right away at first; problem behavior no longer gets the outcome.

78. **FCT to ask for missing puzzle pieces or rings to be put back, for problem behavior maintained by restoring the arrangement** (Anderson & Kenaston-Manasseh, 2024; step 5 of 6; `fct-request-restore-missing-pieces`)  
   After that, prompt only after an error; give no restoration after problem behavior.

79. **Caution card: which FCT changes lessened the return of problem behavior when requests went unanswered** (Mitteer et al., 2026; step 4 of 8; `fct-resurgence-mitigation-comparison`)  
   Give no reinforcer for problem behavior during FCT.

80. **Functional communication training that gives the request a longer, richer reinforcer than problem behavior** (Peck et al., 1996; step 8 of 10; `fct-richer-mand-reinforcement`)  
   Ignore milder problem behavior such as crying or throwing toys.

81. **Functional communication training for aggression when a routine or arrangement is disrupted, with fast delay thinning** (Fernand et al., 2023; step 7 of 10; `fct-routine-delay-probes`)  
   Give no access to the item after aggression, and continue the session.

82. **Functional communication with a card touch, using short 20-second reinforcer periods** (Lerman et al., 2002; step 9 of 10; `fct-short-reinforcer-20s`)  
   In toy sessions, give no toys after self-injury or aggression.

83. **Functional communication training with a sign, extinction and a gradually longer work requirement** (Fisher et al., 1993; step 6 of 11; `fct-sign-extinction-demand-fading`)  
   For item-maintained behavior, do not hand over the item after problem behavior; give it only after the student signs.

84. **Replace crying and whining with a sign for attention, toys or being picked up** (Thompson et al., 2007; step 6 of 8; `fct-sign-replaces-crying`)  
   Give no attention, toys or contact for crying or whining, after checking that basic needs are met.

85. **Teach separate requests for attention and toys, each honored only while its picture is posted** (Fisher et al., 1998a; step 9 of 9; `fct-signaled-attention-toys`)  
   Give no attention, reprimand or toys after destructive behavior and continue the activity.

86. **Functional communication training thinned with signals showing when requests will be honored** (Greer et al., 2016b; step 4 of 10; `fct-signaled-schedule-thinning`)  
   Honor every request and give no reinforcer for destructive behavior; if it occurs just as the reinforcer is due, wait 3 to 5 s before giving it.

87. **FCT with a cardboard keyguard over a speech device so a blind student can feel the buttons (break, eat, tickles), for self-injury with several functions** (Snyder et al., 2024; step 6 of 6; `fct-tactile-keyguard-sgd-visual-impairment`)  
   Give nothing for self-injury.

88. **Teach an "I want to talk" card in place of aggression for favorite topics** (Roscoe et al., 2010; step 7 of 9; `fct-talk-card-topics`)  
   Give no conversation or other attention after aggression, and keep the student and others safe.

89. **FCT for toy-maintained aggression with a picture card or button, before fixed-lean schedule thinning** (Ramirez & Bacotti, 2025; step 6 of 7; `fct-tangible-card-fixedlean`)  
   Do not give the toys after aggression or property destruction.

90. **FCT for two functions in turn: "I want (item) please" for preferred items and "I need help please" for hard tasks, with the wait raised from 2 s to 5 s** (Braithwaite & Richdale, 2000; step 4 of 7; `fct-two-phrases-item-and-help-delay-2-to-5-s`)  
   If self-injury or aggression occurs, give no item or help, prompt the phrase, and honor it.

91. **Teach two ways to ask for a preferred item so a back-up request comes back before problem behavior** (Hoffman & Falcomata, 2014; step 5 of 9; `fct-two-request-forms-backup`)  
   Give no item after aggression or after the other request form.

92. **Teach a separate request for each function: a want sign for items, a break or help card for hard work** (Day et al., 1994; step 5 of 10; `fct-want-and-break-requests`)  
   After problem behavior in this condition, withhold the item and prompt the item request.

93. **Functional communication training with wristband signals that carry to new rooms and staff** (Fisher et al., 2015; step 3 of 10; `fct-wristband-transfer`)  
   Give 20 s with the items and praise for each request, and give no items after problem behavior; for a student who does not use the rule, prompt "Say '[item] please'" after a wait that grows from 0 s to 2 s, 5 s and 10 s.

94. **Whole-class favorite game for few call-outs, plus praise for hand raising** (R. V. Hall et al., 1971; step 5 of 9; `grp_con-class-game-hand-raising`)  
   Answer only students who raise their hands, and do not respond to call-outs.

95. **Whole-class favorite game for few call-outs, plus praise for hand raising** (R. V. Hall et al., 1971; step 9 of 9; `grp_con-class-game-hand-raising`)
