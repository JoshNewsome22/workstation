# Reader rules for the TD-1 and GB-1 banks, and when each one started

The rules the article readers follow when a study is turned into TD-1 protocols and GB-1 objectives, with the release
each rule started in. A bank read before a rule started may have left something out, or written it differently, that
the rule now handles. When every volume is in, the BCBA plans a pass over all the earlier articles under the current
rules; the list at the end is where to start that pass.

Banks in order of reading: Behavior Analysis in Practice and Behavioral Interventions (rp1, rr1, rr2, rr3, bi8688,
pmc), then JABA 1968 to 1991 (v25.20 and before), then JABA 1992 on: 1992 v25.22, 1993 v25.23, 1994 v25.24,
1995 v25.25, 1996 v25.26, 1997 v25.27, 1998 v25.28, 1999 v25.29, 2000 v25.32, 2001 v25.33, 2002 v25.34,
2003 and 2004 v25.35, 2005 v25.36, 2006 v25.37.

## The rules now

1. **Never written**, in a step or an objective: contingent electric shock; aversive sprays, tastes or smells;
   facial screening; physical or mechanical restraint as a consequence; seclusion; contingent exercise or any
   procedure meant to hurt or frighten; food or drink deprivation. Holding a student still, or holding a body part
   as a consequence for problem behavior, is restraint and is never written. If the main procedure is one of these,
   the study is not banked; if it was one part of a package, it is left out and the note says so.
2. **Punishment elements** (response cost, loss of points, privileges or a chosen activity, time-out of any kind,
   overcorrection or positive practice, removing an item as a consequence, reprimands as a consequence): their own
   step, starting "RESTRICTIVE: Punishment (kind); needs approval before use:", with the note that it needs district
   review and written consent and that the card works without it. TD-1 then offers Add without punishment and Add
   with punishment (consent required).
3. **Other restrictive procedures** (blocking or interrupting problem behavior, physical guidance through a demand,
   escape extinction): their own step starting "RESTRICTIVE:", with the note that it needs district review and
   written consent; escape extinction or guidance through a demand also points to TD-1's escape-extinction consent
   block (Responding to behavior sheet).
4. **Holding or guiding a body part inside a teaching procedure** (a physical prompt, the hands guided to the table
   for a few seconds after an error, the hands held down briefly during a trial): its own step starting
   "RESTRICTIVE:", and the note says it needs district review and written consent, stops if the student resists, and
   is never used as a consequence for problem behavior or to make the student eat.
5. **Protective equipment** (padded gloves, mittens, helmets, goggles, arm splints, weights): wearing it is its own
   RESTRICTIVE step, and the note asks for the doctor's input and a plan to fade it.
6. **A school's standing discipline** that ran through every phase of a study (a classroom time-out plan, office
   referrals, bus suspensions) is background, not treatment: it is left off the card and named in the note.
7. **Set aside by default, the BCBA may restore** (no card or objective; support lines may stay): studies of mostly
   adults living in an institution or residential facility. Adults in community, day or vocational programs are
   banked for transition age (14–22 or 18–22), with the note saying it was studied with adults. This default is
   narrower than the BCBA's inclusion rule of 10/28 (v24.78: "don't exclude a protocol unless it's absolutely
   something I wouldn't work on", with cards from every population, residential settings included); the BCBA
   restored Wilder et al. (2001) under it. To settle before the pass: which of the two applies to adults in
   institutions.
8. **Medication:** the card covers only the behavioral procedure; medication decisions belong to the family and the
   doctor.
9. **Medical, dental, feeding and health routines:** planned with the family, the doctor and the school nurse
   (feeding also with a feeding specialist).
10. **Eye contact** is never required: left out or optional, with the caution that many autistic students find it
    aversive.
11. **Food or drink as a reinforcer:** the note says the team decides. Touch, hugs or tickles become what the student
    welcomes.
12. **Words:** "the student" (not child, subject, client, patient or resident); present-day terms (intellectual
    disability) outside reference titles; never "slap", "shock" or "aversive" in a step or a card's name.
13. **No participant names**, real or pseudonym, anywhere on a card or objective.
14. **References:** APA 7 with every author; same-first-author articles of one year keep a/b letters in the key, and
    in the cite only when the cites would otherwise read alike; no year letter in the APA entry; a reference the
    banks already hold is kept as it is.

## When each rule started

| Rule | Started | Banks read before it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Never written (the list) | before v25.20 | none |
| 2 Restrictive and punishment procedures written into the steps (the inclusion rule, 10/28) | v24.78 | the first reading of 1968–2017 (v23.86) left them out; later reads mark them "RESTRICTIVE:" |
| 2 The "RESTRICTIVE: Punishment (kind)" label the two Add buttons look for | v25.26 (JABA 1996) | 1992–1995 brought up to it in v25.31; 1968–1991, BAP, BI, rr1–rr3 and pmc not yet |
| 2 Add with or without punishment in TD-1 | v25.30 | works on any card whose step carries the mark |
| 3 Other restrictive procedures marked | before v25.20 | readers sometimes left guidance through a demand out instead of marking it |
| 4 Teaching holds written as RESTRICTIVE (the BCBA, 10/31) | v25.37 (JABA 2006) | everything before 2006: readers left these holds out |
| 5 Protective equipment step and fade plan | in the briefs from v25.35; added by hand when missing from v25.22 | 1968–1991 and earlier sources not checked |
| 6 Standing discipline is background | v25.34 (Hoch et al., 2002a; a 1994 card done the same way in v25.31) | earlier cards may carry such a step |
| 7 Every population banked (inclusion rule) | v24.78 | the first reading (v23.86) |
| 7 Adults in institutions set aside by default | about v25.10 (JABA 1983) on | conflicts with v24.78; see rule 7 |
| 8 Medication note | in the briefs from v25.35 | earlier cards mostly have it, not always |
| 13 No participant names (scan before each commit) | v25.35; in the briefs from v25.36 | earlier banks not scanned for names |
| 14 Year letters in cites only when needed | v25.35 | earlier cites may carry letters they do not need |

## Known cards for the pass

Holds or guidance left out of a teaching procedure (rule 4), each to get a RESTRICTIVE step and a new note:

- mand_net-incidental-position-words (McGee et al., 1985): the student's hands held at the sides when pointing
  instead of talking.
- choice-store-activity-embedding (Carr & Carlson, 1993): the prompt sequence ended with a hand on the back guiding
  toward the step.
- curr-brief-component-hierarchy (Harding et al., 1994): hand-over-hand guidance through the task.
- peer-modeled-classroom-chains (Werts et al., 1996): the hand guided through the calculator keys.
- dnra-break-with-preferred-activities (Zarcone et al., 1996): physical prompts every 10 s until the task was done.
- s_fr-premack-work-then-favorite (Hanley et al., 2003): the pre-session prompts ended with physical guidance.
- mand_cont-derived-request-cards (Murphy et al., 2005): the hand guided after matching errors.
- p_sf-blocked-color-discrimination (Williams et al., 2005): the hand guided to the table for 3 s after errors.

Other work for the pass:

- Punishment elements in 1968–1991, BAP, BI, rr1–rr3 and pmc written as marked steps (rule 2);
  tools/banks/set-aside-procedures.md lists what was left out of those banks.
- A name scan of every bank (rule 13) and a check of protective-equipment cards before 1992 (rule 5).
- Once rule 7 is settled: the studies of adults in institutions set aside or not banked from v25.10 on (each
  release's notes name them under "Set aside" and "Not banked").
