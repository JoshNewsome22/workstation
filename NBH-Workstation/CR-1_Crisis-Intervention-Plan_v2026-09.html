Deadlines and Parent Notices

§ 1003.573, Florida Statutes (2026), in its own words. (7)(a) A school shall prepare an incident report within 24 hours after a student is released from restraint. If the student’s release occurs on a day before the school closes for the weekend, a holiday, or another reason, the incident report must be completed by the end of the school day on the day the school reopens. (7)(c) A school shall notify the parent or guardian of a student each time restraint is used. Such notification must be in writing and provided before the end of the school day on which the restraint occurs. Reasonable efforts must also be taken to notify the parent or guardian by telephone or e-mail, or both, and these efforts must be documented. The school shall obtain, and keep in its records, the parent’s or guardian’s signed acknowledgment that he or she was notified of his or her child’s restraint. (7)(d) A school shall also provide the parent or guardian with the completed incident report in writing by mail within 3 school days after a student was restrained. The school shall obtain, and keep in its records, the parent’s or guardian’s signed acknowledgment that he or she received a copy of the incident report. (6)(a) Upon the second time a student is restrained during a semester, the school shall develop a crisis intervention plan for the student. (6)(c) The school must provide a copy of the crisis intervention plan to the student’s parent or guardian.

How this is counted. School days are Monday to Friday, less the no-school days listed below. The form knows no district calendar: a holiday, planning day or closure that is not on the list is counted as a school day. District policy may set earlier deadlines than the statute; the form shows the statute’s. The statute does not define a school day or say when the school day ends, so the form never turns “the end of the school day” into a clock time: where a duty ends then, it shows the day and a tick, in the statute’s words, confirms it. Times are this device’s local time. Not shown as a deadline: under (8)(b) the documentation goes to the principal, the district ESE director and the bureau chief “each month that the school is in session,” with no day named; the readiness check on the Debriefing sheet keeps a tick for it.

No-School Days

Weekends are never counted. List each weekday the school is closed to students: holidays, planning days, closures. One day, or a first and last day for a break. The list is saved with this record.

What the Statute Says, as It Now Stands

Summarized from § 1003.573, Florida Statutes (2026), “Seclusion and restraint of students with disabilities in public schools” (last amended in 2023), retrieved September 2026. This is a working summary for planning, not legal advice, and the district’s own policy governs practice. Check the current text before relying on any date or threshold here.

Students coveredA child with an individual education plan, enrolled in kindergarten through grade 12 in a school (as defined in s. 1003.01(17)) or the Florida School for the Deaf and Blind. Not students in prekindergarten, students who reside in residential care facilities under s. 1003.58, or students in a Department of Juvenile Justice education program under s. 1003.52.
SeclusionProhibited. Each district shall prohibit school personnel from using seclusion — the involuntary confinement of a student alone in a room or area and preventing them from leaving. Time-out used as a behavior management technique intended to calm a student is not seclusion.
Mechanical restraintProhibited for school personnel. Does not apply to school resource officers, safety officers, guardians, or security guards acting in their powers and duties with students in grades 6 through 12. Devices prescribed or recommended by physical or behavioral health professionals and used for their indicated purpose are not mechanical restraint.
Physical restraintManual restraint techniques that involve significant physical force to restrict the movement of all or part of a student’s body. Permitted only for authorized school personnel, only when all positive behavior interventions and supports have been exhausted, only when there is an imminent risk of serious injury, and must be discontinued as soon as that threat has dissipated. Only the degree of force necessary. Only to protect the safety of students, school personnel, or others; not for discipline or to correct noncompliance. Techniques may not be used to inflict pain to induce compliance. Briefly holding a student to calm or comfort them, or physically escorting them to a safe location, is not physical restraint.
Crisis intervention planRequired upon the second restraint in a semester. Developed by a team of the parent or guardian, school personnel, and applicable physical and behavioral health professionals. Must contain the specific positive behavior interventions and supports to use, the known physical and behavioral health concerns limiting restraint, and a timetable for review and revision. A copy must be provided to the parent or guardian.
Incident reportWithin 24 hours of release from restraint, or by the end of the day the school reopens if the release falls before a closure. Must include the student’s name, age, grade, ethnicity and disability; date, time and duration; location; type of restraint in the department’s terms; the name of each person restraining or assisting and the date they were last trained in positive behavior interventions and supports; any nonstudent witness; and a description covering the context, the behavior leading up to and precipitating the decision including why there was imminent risk, the interventions used to prevent and de-escalate, what happened immediately afterwards, any injuries, visible marks, or possible medical emergencies (documented according to district policy), evidence of steps to notify the parent, and the date the crisis intervention plan was last reviewed and whether changes were recommended.
Parent notificationEach time restraint is used: in writing before the end of that school day, plus reasonable documented efforts by telephone or e-mail, with the parent’s signed acknowledgment kept. The completed incident report follows by mail within 3 school days, again with a signed acknowledgment kept.
TrainingAnnual, for all personnel authorized to use positive behavior interventions and supports. Must cover the use of those interventions, risk assessment to identify when restraint may be used, examples of when the interventions have failed to reduce imminent risk, safe and appropriate techniques including team use, the district’s documentation and reporting requirements, identifying and dealing with medical emergencies during restraint, and cardiopulmonary resuscitation.
MonitoringAt classroom, building, district, and state level. Documentation goes to the principal, the district ESE director, and the bureau chief of the Bureau of Exceptional Education and Student Services electronically each month school is in session.

How This Form Sits with the Others

The crisis intervention plan is not a behavior intervention plan and does not replace one. The BIP (Form TD-1) addresses the function of the behavior and is what should make restraint unnecessary; this plan governs what happens in the minutes when dangerous behavior is already occurring. A student who needs this form needs the FBA and the BIP reviewed as well — the statute names updating the functional behavioral analysis and the positive behavior intervention plan among the reduction activities.

The Deadline Clock and the Notice Log

On the Restraint log sheet each restraint has its own times, the deadlines § 1003.573 states for it, and a log of the notices to the parent. Each deadline shows as due by, met on, late or overdue since, worked out from the dates and times entered and the time on this device.

Incident report, (7)(a)Due 24 hours after the release, to the minute, with a live countdown on screen (never on paper) once it is less than 24 hours away. When the release falls on a school day followed by a day the school is closed, it is due by the end of the school day on the day the school reopens instead.
Written notice, (7)(c)Due before the end of the school day on which the restraint occurs. The statute does not say when the school day ends, so no clock time is computed: a notice given that day waits for your tick, in the statute’s words, and so does a report completed on the day the school reopens.
Report by mail, (7)(d)Due within 3 school days after the restraint. The form counts from the school day after the restraint; the third is the last day.
Second restraint, (6)(a) and (6)(c)Shown on the second dated restraint on the log, as the statute states them: it gives no number of days for the plan or for the copy to the parent.
School daysMonday to Friday, less the no-school days you list. The form knows no district calendar, and district policy may set earlier deadlines than the statute. How the form counts school days and reads a closure is this form’s working convention: the statute does not define a school day.
Notice logThe written notice, each telephone or e-mail attempt (date, time, method and outcome), the parent’s signed acknowledgment, the date the report was mailed, and the signed acknowledgment of the report. Now and Today fill in the moment of entry (in a date or time field, the N key does the same). A date typed in the log’s Date column stays as typed; where it reads plainly as month/day/year, one click copies it into the restraint’s own date.
Readiness check and cardsWith the debriefed restraint chosen on the Debriefing sheet, each readiness item the notice log records is read from it; an item it does not record keeps its tick, so a record made before the log existed reads as it always did. The cards count from the times entered where they decide a deadline, and from the log’s Report filed column otherwise.

The clinical content of this form — the intervention sequence, the precursor, the debriefing questions, and which health professionals the team must include — is a working convention. The statutory requirements quoted above are the law as retrieved; everything else is practice.
