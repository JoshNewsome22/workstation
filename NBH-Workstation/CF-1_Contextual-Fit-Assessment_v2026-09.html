What Contextual Fit Is, and What It Is Not

Contextual fit is the extent to which a support plan matches the values, skills, and resources of the people who will run it and the settings where it will run. It is a property of the relationship between a plan and a context, not a property of the plan and not a judgment of the staff. A technically excellent plan can have poor contextual fit, and fit affects whether a plan is run as written: after a protocol designed to improve fit was introduced, implementation fidelity rose and problem behavior fell (Monzalve & Horner, 2021, with four participants). Poor fit is therefore one likely reason a well-designed plan produces no change.

This form is not a satisfaction survey and not an evaluation of the implementer. It asks what would have to be true for the plan to actually run, and records where those conditions are absent. The answer to a low score is a change to the plan or to the supports around it, not pressure on the person who gave the score.

Respondent pages. Ratings given independently are the point of this form, and Respondent pages in the toolbar makes that easy: a one-page questionnaire with the 21 items for one respondent and one round, as a file to attach to an email or as a link when the workstation is on a website. How the Figures Are Calculated

Item meanThe mean rating across respondents who answered that item.
Domain meanThe mean of all answered ratings within the domain, across respondents. Unanswered items are excluded rather than scored as neutral.
Overall fitThe mean of every answered rating on the form, across items and respondents, so a domain with more items counts for more. The published Self-Assessment of Contextual Fit is scored differently: a mean for each two-item subscale, and a total that is the mean of the subscale means (Horner, Salentine & Albin, 2003).
Fit bandNamed from the overall fit: strong at 5.0 or above; adequate from 4.0 to below 5.0 (with named gaps when an item is flagged); poor from 3.0 to below 4.0; very poor below 3.0. The bands are a working convention, not a finding. Whatever the band, the verdict names the flagged items, and it shows green only when no item is flagged and every rating is in. Its advice assumes the plan has not started unless “When this was administered” says otherwise.
Ratings enteredEach respondent listed can give one rating per item in each round. The verdict counts the ratings entered and the respondents who gave them; while any are missing, the band is provisional and the verdict gives no go-ahead. When the two rounds were not rated by the same respondents on the same items, the change between them is marked as not like for like.
Flagged itemAny item on which at least one respondent rated at or below the threshold (a whole number from 1 to 5, set in the toolbar), regardless of the item mean. The Enhancement sheet generates its rows from the items flagged in round 1.
SpreadThe range between the highest and lowest rating on an item. A spread of 3 or more is reported separately, because averaging hides it.

Writing help. A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print. A respondent’s own words belong in quotation marks, which Check wording leaves alone.

About the simulation. Load simulation fills every sheet with a simulated administration that describes no real student or staff: 21 items, 4 respondents, two rounds, 16 enhancement actions, and the comparison against obtained integrity. Use Clear all before entering a case.

Sources for the construct: Albin, Lucyshyn, Horner & Flannery (1996), who defined contextual fit for behavior support plans; Horner, Salentine & Albin (2003) for the Self-Assessment of Contextual Fit in Schools, from which the domain structure and the six-point scale here are adapted (its 16 items form eight two-item subscales; this form keeps knowledge, skills, values, resources, administrative support and effectiveness, has no domain for the student’s best interest or for efficiency, and adds fit with the routine); the published instrument’s measurement properties have had little study (Monzalve, 2016, reported that no systematic study had documented them; Coyle et al., 2022, examined a 12-item adaptation for HIV care settings); Monzalve & Horner (2021) for the contextual fit enhancement procedure and its effect on implementation fidelity. The items on this form are written for this setting and are not the published instrument; the thresholds and bands are a working convention, not a finding. Where a formal score is needed for research or district reporting, administer the published Self-Assessment of Contextual Fit instead.
