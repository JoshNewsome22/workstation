How the Level of Evidence Is Derived

The band is set by the best method tier that was actually conducted and that supported the stated function, then reduced if the sources disagree. It is a working convention for keeping the report's language proportionate to its design, not an empirical finding. The form applies it as follows.

  • What counts. A source counts toward a function when its method tests function (tiers 1–4 below) and it has a function and a strength. A source recorded as Unknown assessed function but identified none: it is counted among the sources that assessed function, not as support. Preference assessments, reinforcer assessments and treatment integrity observations are listed with the methods but carry no tier: they do not test what maintains the behavior, and are not counted.
  • Tier. A function's tier is the best tier among the sources rated Strong or Moderate that support it. A Weak rating counts as support but does not set the tier, so a function supported only by Weak ratings is at level 4.
  • The function the level describes. The best-supported function: the best tier first, then the most sources rated Strong, then the most supporting sources. A function that no source supports is never reported, however often it is contradicted.
  • Reductions. One level if any source contradicts that function, and one level if another function has at least as many supporting sources. The level does not fall below 4.
  • Summary statements. Each target's statement ends with its hypothesized function at the level of evidence for that function: “The function is…” only at level 1, “The hypothesized function is…” with its level at levels 2–4.
Tier 1Experimental. A functional analysis with a demonstration of experimental control — differentiated responding across conditions, replicated. Licenses causal language: the behavior is maintained by.
Tier 2Structured descriptive with a contingency analysis. Conditional probabilities compared against base rates, or a systematic non-experimental isolation. Licenses contingency language: the consequence is differentially associated with the behavior. It does not establish that manipulating it will change the behavior.
Tier 3Descriptive observation. ABC narrative, scatter plot, daily rate data. Licenses correlational language only: the behavior occurs more often when.
Tier 4Indirect. Interviews, rating scales, records. Licenses report language only: informants describe the behavior as.
No tierNot a test of function. Preference assessment, reinforcer assessment, treatment integrity observation. Recorded for the plan; they license no statement about what maintains the behavior.

Convergence across several tier-4 sources raises confidence that the informants agree. It does not raise the tier. Three interviews agreeing on escape is three reports, not a demonstration.

A component functional analysis (Bann & Morris, 2026) is listed at tier 2 as a working convention of this form: its authors state that its evocative and reinforcement probabilities should not be the sole basis for a function conclusion until the method is replicated and its cutoffs are applied prospectively, and in their nine cases the result was confirmed by an extended multielement analysis, which is entered as its own tier-1 row when it has been run. The higher-level RRB analysis of Fergus et al. (2026) is a multielement functional analysis and is entered as one.

What the Report States for the 2026 Analysis Formats

When the experimental analysis used one of the formats on the evidence sheet’s detail table, the report’s experimental-analysis section states the arrangement and the outcome in the terms the source used, so that a reader can judge the result against the published procedure.

  • Arranging and ordering (Fergus et al., 2026). The format (multielement) and conditions; whether no-interaction or alone was used and why; the extended alone series if run; the baiting arrangement (number and kinds of disrupted items, identical across conditions); the reading rule (undifferentiated including no-interaction, or persistent in the extended series, read as automatic); whether a social function was ruled out; the process-versus-product result and whether it informed treatment; the A-CSA phase at which three or more effective competing stimuli emerged, the stimuli by name, and the treatment level that phase prescribes (none, prompting, or prompting plus blocking); whether blocking was later withdrawn; and the outcome in the source’s units: episodes per minute against baseline, functional item engagement, prompts and blocks per session, emotional responses, and the generalization setting and result. An ECS is an item associated with at least an 80% reduction in RRB relative to a no-stimulus control.
  • Component FA (Bann & Morris, 2026). The number of sessions completed; the evocative and reinforcement probabilities for each isolated and combined component; which cutoff was applied (.50 evocative and .25 reinforcement; .33 evocative with exactly three sessions); whether a no-interaction screen preceded it; whether an extended multielement analysis confirmed the result, with its SVI quotient; and the statement that component-FA probabilities alone are not yet a validated basis for a function conclusion.
  • Validity caveats. Whether interobserver agreement and procedural fidelity were collected; concurrent treatments; therapist changes; and any assessment stopped early for escalation. Fergus et al. reported agreement from 0% to 100% and no fidelity data, changed a therapist who was concurrently being trained on another RRB treatment, and ran one participant’s analysis alongside an FCT program; Bann and Morris halted one synthesized test after escalation that lasted hours.

The Summary Statement and the Pathway

The summary statement is written in the order the events occur: setting event, then antecedent, then behavior, then maintaining consequence. Writing it in that order forces each element to be filled and makes an incomplete hypothesis visible.

The competing behavior pathway places three routes against the same antecedent. The top route is the desired behavior and the consequence it currently produces, which is usually delayed or weak — that is why it loses. The middle route is the problem behavior and the consequence that maintains it. The bottom route is the alternative behavior, which must produce the same consequence as the problem behavior, sooner and for less effort, or it will not compete.
