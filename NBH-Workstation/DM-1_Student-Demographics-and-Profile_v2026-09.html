Guide: What This Form Holds and How It Is Used

Assessment forms should describe the assessment; the facts about the student that every form needs should live in one place. This form is that place. It is completed once when the FBA begins, reviewed at each IEP or reevaluation, and updated when a contact, medication, release, or team member changes; the date of the last review is on the packet sheet. It is not an assessment: nothing on it is interpreted, and behavior data belong on the other forms.

1. What Goes Here and What Does Not

EvidenceUnder FERPA (34 CFR Part 99) this form, once the school maintains it, is part of the student's education record (§99.3): the parent, or the eligible student, must be given the opportunity to inspect and review it (§99.10), and personally identifiable information from it is disclosed only with a signed and dated written consent that specifies the records, states the purpose, and identifies the party or class of parties to whom they may be disclosed (§99.30), except as §99.31 allows (for example, to school officials with legitimate educational interests). IDEA requires the agency to protect the confidentiality of personally identifiable information (34 CFR §§300.610–300.627), to keep a record of access (§300.614), and to obtain parental consent before disclosing it to parties other than officials of participating agencies, unless the information is in education records and FERPA authorizes the disclosure without consent (§300.622(a)). The BACB Ethics Code (2020) requires behavior analysts to protect confidential information, including documentation and data (Standard 2.03), to share with a third party only information critical to the purpose of the communication (2.04), to meet applicable requirements for storing, retaining, and destroying documentation (2.05), and to create and maintain detailed, high-quality documentation of their professional activities (3.11). Under Florida Rule 6A-6.03311(8), when a student with a disability reaches 18 the rights afforded to parents transfer to the student, except the right to notice, which the parent and the student then share; this does not apply to a student determined incompetent under State law or for whom a guardian advocate has been appointed to make educational decisions. At least one year before the student reaches 18, the school must give the student and the parent information and instruction on self-determination and on the rights that transfer, including a written notice (Rule 6A-6.03311(8)(d)); a guardian advocate under Florida Statute §393.12 may hold educational decision-making without a full guardianship.
Applied guidanceRecord what the behavior-support team needs and can show a reason for: identity, the rights-holder and who may be contacted, the team, program and eligibility, health facts and medications that act on behavior, diagnoses as documented in the file, safety precautions, and releases. Race and ethnicity are entered only if the district's reporting requires it on this document; otherwise leave the field blank and rely on the student information system. Diagnoses are copied from a document on file with its source and date, never from memory or hearsay. A contact's "may be told about" is a school-side note of what the rights-holder has authorized, not a substitute for a signed release.

2. Educational Rights and Contact Permissions

  • Rights-holder. Mark exactly who can consent (IC-1) and receive notices: parent, both parents (shared), legal guardian, a foster parent (34 CFR §300.30(a)(2)), a person acting as parent with whom the child lives, such as a grandparent, stepparent or other relative (§300.30(a)(4)), surrogate parent, guardian advocate, or the adult student (from 18 the right to notice is shared by the student and the parent, Rule 6A-6.03311(8)(a)). A court order or other legally binding document that limits a parent's rights is noted with its date and location in the file; unless the school has evidence of a court order, State statute, or legally binding document that specifically revokes them, either parent has full rights under FERPA (§99.4).
  • May be told about. A relative or family friend may be listed as a contact the rights-holder has authorized for day-to-day communication: pick-up and scheduling, or emergencies. Telling them about the behavior, the plan or the data is a disclosure from the education record and needs the rights-holder’s signed and dated release naming the records, the purpose and the person (§99.30): record its date on the contact, or the card says “Logistics only”. A relative the student lives with may be acting as a parent (§99.3); if so, say so in Educational rights and in the rights-holder above. Leave the answer blank for a contact who holds educational rights. That permission does not extend to records, which need the district release.
  • Age of majority. The form computes the student's age from the date of birth. From 16½ it names the 17th birthday, by which (at least one year before 18) the school must give the student and the parent information and instruction on the rights that transfer at 18, including a written notice (Rule 6A-6.03311(8)(d)), and the IEP must state that the student has been informed of those rights (34 CFR §300.320(c)); from 17 it reports whether that notice is recorded; and from 17½ it flags the 18th birthday so the team can plan the notice of the transfer (Rule 6A-6.03311(8)(c)) and, where a guardian advocate or guardianship is planned, start the process before the birthday. The IEP statement, the notice given at 18 (34 CFR §300.520(a)(3)) and how decisions will be made from 18 have their own fields; the steps still open go to the workstation’s due list and calendar, and Draft the letter on the rights at 18 writes a letter to the family from the contacts and the home language, a draft for the ESE contact to check.
  • Home language and interpreter. The parent must be fully informed, in his or her native language or other mode of communication, of the information relevant to any consent sought (34 CFR §300.9(a)), and written notice must be provided in the parent's native language or other mode of communication unless it is clearly not feasible to do so (§300.503(c)); record the language and whether an interpreter is needed so every form after this one gets it right. Form IC-1 takes the language, the interpreter need and the rights-holder (as its signer) from the case, and says when its consent records no interpreter in the family’s language.

3. Health, Medication, and Diagnosis Entries

  • Medications are listed as the health record and the district medication authorization show them, with dose, route, time given at school, prescriber, and the date of the last change. The last-change date is what the assessment forms align against (RR-1, MS-1); it is the reason this table exists, and the start and last-change dates go to those forms with the case. Every change, dated, goes in the Medication Changes table under it; with the start and last-change dates it goes to the case, and Form DD-1 marks each change on its graphs, so a change in behavior can be read against the change in medication.
  • Diagnoses are entered with a code picker (ICD-10-CM codes as used with DSM-5-TR) to keep wording consistent, plus the source document, who made the diagnosis, and its date. A diagnosis reported verbally by a parent is entered with "parent report" as the source and no code until a document arrives.
  • Medical protocols (seizure, allergy, asthma, diet, feeding, mobility) are named with the date of the nurse's plan; the protocol itself stays with the nurse.

4. The Profile and the Packet

The Profile sheet is the one place in the packet where the student is described as a person rather than as a set of behaviors: strengths, interests, how they communicate yes, no, and pain, what helps, what to avoid, and the safety precautions every adult must know. It is written with the parent and, where possible, the student. The Packet sheet lists the forms in this student's file with dates, so a reader can see what has been done and what is current, and records the releases and consents that authorize it. Inside the workstation the list follows the workstation’s own forms and ticks In file for each form the case holds work on. The status answers typed at intake (the BIP and crisis plan, the photo permission) carry the date they were last confirmed, which the workstation repeats with them; when Forms CR-1, TD-1 and IC-1 hold the facts, an answer still empty or No or Pending takes them, dated and noted in the change log.

Writing help

A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print.

Statements about FERPA and IDEA paraphrase 34 CFR Parts 99 and 300; Florida rules and statutes are cited by number. Diagnostic codes follow ICD-10-CM as listed in DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association, 2022); the code library on the Health sheet is a convenience list and is not exhaustive. Verify against the current code set and district procedures.
