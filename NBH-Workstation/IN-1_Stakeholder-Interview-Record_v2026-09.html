What the Respondents Agree and Disagree About

Answers are laid side by side on the questions that bear on function. Agreement across people who see the student in different places is the useful signal; a disagreement is not noise to be averaged away but a finding to record and, where possible, test directly.

Why Interview, and What It Can and Cannot Give You

An interview serves five purposes (O'Neill et al., 2015):

  • Specify and confirm target behaviors.
  • Identify the specific conditions under which target behaviors are most likely and least likely to occur.
  • Identify potential antecedents, setting events, and lifestyle factors that are influencing the student's behavior.
  • Provide a broader perspective than that achieved by direct observation.
  • Provide an opportunity to establish rapport with school personnel and families.

Conduct interviews face to face with people who know the student well and interact with them across a range of settings and situations. Questions may need to be repeated and clarified for some respondents, and follow-up questions should be asked to encourage thoughtful, open communication.

What this is worth as evidence. Interviews are an indirect assessment. On the level-of-evidence scheme used in Form FS-1 they sit at tier 4: they license statements about what informants describe, not about what maintains the behavior. Agreement among four informants raises confidence that the informants agree; it does not raise the tier. Treat the hypothesis that comes out of this form as the thing to examine, not as the finding: direct observation on Forms ABC-1 and SP-1 adds correlations that help decide what a functional analysis on Form EA-1 tests.

The Three Question Sets

Parent or guardianHome-focused, and opens by asking the parent to describe their child rather than the problem. Includes two Yes / No / Sometimes matrices: contributing health and lifestyle factors, and the request and routine conditions under which the behavior is more likely. Five questions added in this revision ask how the child communicates and asks for things, when the behavior started and how it has changed, whether it happens when the child is alone, how hard it is and how long it lasts, and why the parent thinks it happens.
Teacher, staff member, related service providerSchool-focused, covering the behavior, strengths and skill deficits, when and where it is most and least likely, triggers, precursors, what follows it, the respondent's own view of function, what has already been tried, and what works proactively. Added in this revision: how the student communicates and asks, what the student works for, the history, whether it happens when the student is alone, how hard and how long, and a Yes / No / Sometimes checklist of setting events on the school day.
StudentFirst person and strengths-first, asked in plain language. Preferences and dislikes, what is easy and hard, when things go best, what they want to work for, what happens around the behavior, what they notice beforehand, what helps them calm down, and, added in this revision, what they do when they need help or want a break. The student’s own interview for the report, with the assent plan and the student’s hypothesis, is Form SI-1: use this set when the district form is required, after assent is recorded there. Replace the words [target behavior] with the student's own word for it before asking.

Writing help

A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print. The answer boxes of the interview have no button: an answer is the respondent’s report, kept in the words they used, so it is not checked or rewritten. The boxes where you sum up (what the respondents agree and disagree about) have it; quote a respondent’s own words there, in quotation marks, which Check wording leaves alone.
