Guide: Briefing Staff Who See the Student Briefly

1. Why This Is Not a Shorter Behavior Plan

A bus attendant, a music teacher and a lunchroom monitor are not running the intervention. They are trying not to make the situation worse, to recognize the request when it comes, and to know when to call. Handing them the plan, or a condensed version of it, produces one of two failures: they read none of it, or they attempt procedures they cannot run in their setting and run them badly.

So this document is selected, not summarized. Most of the plan is deliberately absent.

2. What Must Never Be Left Out

Two blocks earn their place on every brief regardless of setting. The first is what not to do: limited-contact staff rarely cause harm by omitting a procedure, and regularly cause harm by doing the one thing that strengthens the behavior — arguing, or removing the demand at the wrong moment — or by holding a student. A hold that uses significant physical force to restrict a student’s movement is physical restraint, which Florida law permits only for authorized school personnel, only when all positive behavior interventions and supports have been exhausted and there is an imminent risk of serious injury; seclusion is prohibited outright (§ 1003.573(1)(d)2., (2) and (3)(b), Florida Statutes). What trained staff do is on the crisis plan (Form CR-1). Unless everyone receiving the brief is named there as trained, the brief tells them when and whom to call, not how to hold. The second is the threshold for calling: a person who does not know when to call will either call for everything or for nothing.

3. Honor the Request Every Time

If the plan teaches a request, the people who see the student occasionally are the ones most likely to ignore it — they were not there when it was taught and do not know what it cost to establish. A request that works in the classroom and not on the bus is on extinction on the bus, and when a request taught by functional communication training stops working, the problem behavior it replaced tends to return (Volkert et al., 2009). If that behavior then gets the student what the request would have, it is being reinforced again. Say so on the page, in plain words: every time, even the third time.

4. Write for the Setting, Not for the Chart

A driver cannot leave the seat. A music teacher has thirty other students and no adult support. A brief that asks for something the setting cannot deliver is not a brief, it is a liability. Where the plan's procedure cannot run here, say what to do instead rather than leaving the line in.

5. Keep It to Pages, Not Sections

One page is better than two, and two are better than three. The length line on The brief view estimates the printed length by laying the brief out as it will print; the acknowledgment page is not counted, because it stays with you rather than with the reader.

6. Who May Receive It

This is part of the student's education record. It goes to staff with a legitimate educational interest in working with this student, and no further. Name the setting and the roles on the brief so that scope is on the page, and collect the acknowledgment so you know who holds a copy.

Writing help

A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite; it is Apple’s own and works on the text as written, names included, on the iPad or on Apple’s servers, so follow your agency’s rules for it and do not use its ChatGPT options for student text. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print. Concise keeps every fact in fewer words, which suits a brief that has to fit a page.
