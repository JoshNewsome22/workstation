Training Components Delivered

All four components are recorded because behavior skills training is defined by the package. Training that relies on lectures and written material builds knowledge but has often failed to teach staff to perform new job skills (as reviewed by Parsons, Rollyson & Reid, 2012); in a component analysis with teachers learning to conduct functional analyses, rehearsal alone was ineffective, modeling improved performance for some teachers, and feedback improved it for all of them (Ward-Horner & Sturmey, 2012). Hard Cases Rehearsed

Rehearsal should include the moments that produce consequence errors: a resetting DRO or a delay needs continuous monitoring, timer resets and reinforcer delivery with no natural prompt, so an omitted reinforcer after a successful interval and a reinforcer delivered after the target behavior are the likely error forms, above all for an implementer whose attention is divided (O’Neill et al., 2026); in discrimination training the consequence that follows an incorrect response is the critical risk point (Burlison et al., 2026). How often each error occurs in practice has not been described, so tick what was rehearsed and note the rounds; the Role-play with error arrangement is where these belong.

How This Form Is Meant to Run

Behavior skills training is a package: describe the skill, model it, have the trainee rehearse it, and give immediate feedback, repeating the rehearsal-and-feedback loop until a stated criterion is met. The criterion is the point of the procedure. Training that ends when the session ends, rather than when performance reaches a standard, is what produces staff who have been trained and still cannot run the plan.

The rehearsal grid uses the same four codes as the integrity form, so the same protocol is scored the same way whether the trainee is role-playing or working with the student. Errors of commission and omission are separated for the same reason as on TI-1: a step that is attempted incorrectly needs more rehearsal, while a step that is never attempted usually needs a cue.

The rehearsal criterion has two parts. The overall criterion, the percentage and the consecutive rounds set in the toolbar (90% across two rounds by default), is this form’s convention. The consequence gate is stricter: every step tagged Consequence on the Protocol steps sheet must be scored and correct on every opportunity in the decision rounds, with zero commission errors, before the trainee runs the plan without the trainer present. The gate follows the direction of the evidence rather than a tested training variable: in a parametric test of consequence errors during discrimination training, reinforcing a wrong response was the more detrimental error, with a 0.24–0.29 probability of a correct response on the next trial against 0.46–0.63 after earned reinforcement was withheld, and the authors recommend evidence-based preservice training and ongoing coaching so that critical components are not delivered with low and varying fidelity (Burlison et al., 2026); in DRO, commission errors were more detrimental than omission errors, and experiencing full fidelity before reduced fidelity, and gradual rather than abrupt reductions, had a protective effect (Hronek & Kestner, 2025, as summarized by O’Neill et al., 2026); in DRA, programmed errors were less detrimental when the procedure had first been run with perfect fidelity (St. Peter Pipkin et al., 2010, as summarized by Burlison et al., 2026). Learners with a history of consequence errors still mastered the task once the errors were corrected (Burlison et al., 2026), so a trainee found in error on TI-1 is retrained on this form, not taken off the plan. Neither study tested a 100% rehearsal criterion, or behavior skills training itself, and a trainee at criterion in rehearsal has not been shown to hold fidelity in situ, which is why the in-situ probe and the early TI-1 re-check remain.

Sign-off ends the training, not the supervision: “Competent” on this form means the trainee may run the plan without the trainer present. For an RBT, the RBT Ethics Code also requires that behavior-technician services be provided only after the supervisor confirms the RBT has demonstrated competence, and only within a clearly defined role under close, ongoing supervision (Behavior Analyst Certification Board, 2021, standards 1.06 and 1.03).

The Rehearsal Runner

On the Rehearsal sheet, Set up a round offers the next empty round, or one you choose, with its arrangement, and fills in the date from this device’s calendar. Code each step as the trainee performs it with Correct, Commission, Omission or N/A, one step at a time or with all steps listed (keys C, M, O and N; the arrow keys move between steps, Z undoes the last code and Space pauses the round timer), with the round’s accuracy against the mastery criterion, and the critical steps correct, shown as you go. A step that comes due more than once in a round is coded each time and written to the grid as Form TI-1’s runner writes it: Commission if any of those times was a commission error, otherwise Omission if any was an omission error, otherwise Correct. Like the other rules on this form, that is a working convention, not a finding.

End round lists the steps not coded and gives a feedback prompt built from that round’s own codes: each step not performed correctly, critical steps first, with its description and whether the error was one of commission or omission, then the steps performed correctly. Parsons, Rollyson & Reid (2012) recommend feedback following each role play, describing what the trainee performed correctly and specifying what was not. Save to the round writes its codes, date and arrangement, and the feedback you record, through the grid’s own fields, so every figure and the decision are recalculated as for hand coding, and ticks, as you choose, the components the round delivered under Training Components Delivered. Replacing a round that holds entries, and Discard, ask first. After steps to retrain are imported from Form TI-1, the runner offers them first, as a round of those steps only; the other steps are left blank in that round’s column. The runner never prints.

Importing From Form TI-1

Import From TI-1, on the Protocol steps sheet and under In-Situ Probe, reads Form TI-1’s record in one of three ways: inside the workstation, from the TI-1 open there; from the retraining list TI-1 saves for this form (Save retraining list for ST-1); or from the data file TI-1 saves with Save data. Any other file is refused, and nothing on this form changes. You choose which parts to take: TI-1’s protocol steps with their critical marks, to set up this form’s step list; the steps to retrain, marked Retrain on the step list with their error pattern and offered first by the rehearsal runner; and the in-situ probe.

The steps to retrain are those below TI-1’s criterion, or with any commission or omission error, on its deciding observations: the last unannounced observations with scored steps, as many as its criterion counts. Each is matched to this form’s step of the same number and description; one that does not match is named, not marked. The in-situ probe is the latest of those observations. Its date, integrity and critical-step integrity go into the In-Situ Probe fields, so the decision uses TI-1’s own figures, and it is offered only when it is dated on or after the last scored rehearsal round. Nothing is replaced without a keep-or-replace choice. Where each part came from (the workstation or the file) and the date of the import are recorded with this form, shown and printed under the step list and the probe, and kept by Save data and by the workstation.

How the Figures Are Calculated

Round accuracyWithin one rehearsal round: correct steps ÷ scored steps, with N/A removed from the denominator. A round with no scored step is left out of every figure; a round marked In-situ practice or In-situ probe is counted like any other round. The probe that clears the trainee is the one recorded on the Competency sheet from TI-1.
Critical-step accuracyThe same calculation restricted to steps marked critical, pooled across the decision rounds: once the criterion has been met, the latest rounds it counts (the latest two when it asks for two consecutive rounds); before that, every scored round.
Rounds to criterionThe number of scored rounds up to and including the round in which the criterion was first met on the required number of consecutive rounds. The card and the verdict name that round as it is labelled on the grid.
Per-step accuracyFor each step, the percentage of rounds in which it was performed correctly, out of the rounds where it applied.
Consequence gateOver the decision rounds, for the steps tagged Consequence: each must be scored there and correct every time (100%, zero commission and zero omission errors). A consequence step not scored in those rounds has not been shown, and the decision says so. The gate is this form’s convention, informed by Burlison et al. (2026), O’Neill et al. (2026) and the work they summarize; the card on the Competency sheet also shows commission errors on consequence steps across every round.
Competency decisionCriterion met on the required consecutive rounds and still met in the latest rounds it counts, and every step marked critical scored in those rounds and correct every time, and an unannounced in-situ probe recorded with the integrity obtained on TI-1 at or above the criterion percentage. Parsons, Rollyson & Reid (2012) suggest a mastery criterion under which critical steps are performed at 100%; holding the in-situ probe to the rehearsal percentage is this form’s convention. When the critical-step integrity obtained on TI-1 is recorded for the probe (Import From TI-1 fills it), it must be 100%: every critical step scored on the probe correct, as in the decision rounds. That, too, is this form’s convention.

Writing help. A multi-line text box on this form shows an Improve wording button at its corner when you are in it and whenever it holds text; from a keyboard, Alt+Enter in the box (Option+Return on a Mac or iPad) opens the same panel. Check wording works on this device and sends nothing: it marks words that name a feeling, guess at intent, function or a diagnosis, label the behavior or the person, or leave a count, time or intensity vague, and suggests observable wording; words in quotation marks are left alone. Rewrite with Claude works only in the forms opened from the website: a single-use passcode from your BCBA unlocks the tab (in the workstation, every form in that tab) until the session’s time is up or the tab is closed; press Lock when you finish on a shared iPad. Before anything is sent, the student’s name and ID become placeholders such as [Student], other names can be added under Also hide, and the panel shows exactly what will be sent; the names are put back in the answer. Use it for real cases only with your district’s or agency’s approval. On an iPad with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools (select the text) can also proofread and rewrite. The button and the panel are not saved with the form and never print. The text here is about a staff member: add their name under Also hide before you send (the panel hides the student’s name and ID by itself and points out other names it finds).

About the simulation. Load simulation fills every sheet with a simulated training record that describes no real trainee or student: 14 steps tagged by component, 5 rehearsal rounds with the hard cases noted, acquisition curve, the consequence gate, and a competency decision with the in-situ probe and the first TI-1 re-check recorded. Use Clear all before entering a record.
