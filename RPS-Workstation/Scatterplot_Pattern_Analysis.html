How to Mark a Cell

Blank — the behavior did not occur in this interval.
Slash — low rate (below the cutoff set above).
Shaded — high rate (at or above the cutoff).
X — not observed (absent, pulled out, no sheet): left out of every count.

Click a cell to move it blank → slash → shaded. Arrow keys move between cells; 0, 1 and 2 set a cell directly.

Scatter plot method after Touchette, P. E., MacDonald, R. F., & Langer, S. N. (1985). A scatter plot for identifying stimulus control of problem behavior. Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis, 18(4), 343–351. In the original procedure the slash denoted a single occurrence within the interval and the filled cell denoted more than one; the low-rate / high-rate cutoff above is the common applied adaptation and is set by the observer, not by the source.

A scatter plot shows when the behavior occurred, not why. Temporal clustering points to conditions that co-vary with the interval — an activity, a person, a demand, a deprivation state — and those are hypotheses to test, not findings. Kahng et al. (1998, JABA, 31, 593–604) found that visual inspection of 15 scatter plots showed no reliable temporal pattern in any of them, while statistical control charts of the same data showed one or more 30-min intervals in which problem behavior was more likely in 12, so treat a flat profile as inconclusive rather than as evidence of no pattern, and confirm any pattern against ABC data or a functional analysis before writing it into the behavior plan.
