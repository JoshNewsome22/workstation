The Performance Concern

One concern per form, about the employee’s specific performance concern rather than the employee in general, operationally defined as a behavioral excess or deficit. A clear pinpoint lets you phrase every question concretely (“have you been trained to run the error-correction procedure” rather than “trained on the task”). Conduct a new form for a different concern.

The PDC–HS Interview

Instructions (PDC–HS 1.1). Conduct as an interview with the employee’s direct supervisor. Do not simply hand it to the supervisor and ask them to complete it by themselves, unless they are well trained in completing the PDC–HS. Answer the questions about the employee’s specific performance concern (not the employee in general). Complete for only one performance concern at a time. Answer the questions in the order in which they appear. If the direct supervisor is unsure about what is being asked in a question, provide clarification or examples, and record what you added in the note.

Items marked * should be answered only after the information is verified through direct observation or interview with the employee. Record how each was verified; the Observations sheet holds the log.

Answering NO vs. N/A. Answer NO when the information required by the question is not an obvious or immediate “Yes.” Answer N/A when the information required by the question does not apply or is irrelevant to the performance concern being assessed. N/A is offered only on the items that allow it.

Verification: Observations and the Staff Member’s Account

Eight items carry an asterisk and are answered only after direct observation or an interview with the staff member. Supervisor report alone is not enough for them: informant accounts of why behavior happens are accurate in only about 64% of cases (Iwata et al., 2013, as cited in Brand et al., 2022), and supervisors who are good at naming a staff member’s favorite item still rank preferences poorly (Wilder et al., 2007, 2011). Log each observation here and set “Verified by” on the item.

Tips for conducting direct observations (PDC–HS 1.1). Attempt to conduct observations during typical conditions (e.g., the employee is not sick or unduly affected by things like low staffing or unfamiliar clients). Conduct observations during times when the employee is typically expected to be performing the task. Be aware of employee reactivity; employees may behave differently when they know they are being observed. Conduct only as many observations as necessary to obtain reliable information.

What the PDC–HS Is

Performance analysis is the organizational equivalent of the functional assessment of problem behavior (Brand, Sellers, Wilder & Carr, 2022). When a plan component is not being run, the question is the same one an FBA asks about a student: what in the environment is keeping the behavior from happening? The Performance Diagnostic Checklist–Human Services (PDC–HS; Carr, Wilder, Majdalany, Mathisen & Strain, 2013) is an informant-based tool designed to assess the environmental variables that contribute to poor employee performance in human service settings, adapted from Austin’s (2000) Performance Diagnostic Checklist for business settings. The original has 20 questions in four domains, 13 informant-based and 7 requiring direct observation. This form reproduces version 1.1, the revision used in the authors’ training videos, which has 22 items and flags 8 for verification by observation or by interviewing the staff member. Version 1.1 is an unpublished training document; the published instrument and the evidence are the 2013 version and the studies cited below.

It is not a performance review and not a judgement of the staff member. It asks the direct supervisor what conditions for the task are present and which are missing, and it pairs each missing condition with an intervention that has evidence behind it. Re-explaining the plan and asking the aide to try harder is a training-domain intervention chosen without a diagnosis. In the cases where Wilder et al. (2018) tried an intervention the checklist had not indicated, it produced no change, and the indicated one then did.

What the Evidence Supports

Each domain can be the causeWilder, Lipschultz and Gehrman (2018) ran the PDC–HS on six therapists in an autism clinic and found a different leading domain for different people: training (75% of items NO), task clarification and prompting (40%), resources and materials (66.5%), and consequences (40% and 50%). An intervention drawn from the leading domain improved performance in every case.
The indicated intervention beats a guessWhere a non-indicated intervention was tried first (extra materials, a written task clarification), it produced no change; the indicated one then did. Mand opportunities rose from 0.01 to 1.02 per minute with graphed feedback, from 0.02 to 0.68 per minute with behavioral skills training, and timer use rose from 0% to 100% of sessions once the timer was simply placed on the clipboard (Wilder et al., 2018). The authors’ summaries of Carr et al. (2013) and Ditzian et al. (2015) show the same pattern for therapy-room upkeep and door closing. Only three non-indicated interventions were tested; the authors note that a feedback and goal-setting package might have done as well.
Scoring agreementA second rater scoring recorded interviews agreed 100% in Wilder et al. (2018). Brand et al. (2022) describe the tool as having acceptable reliability and validity, referring the reader to the review by Wilder, Cymbal and Villacorta (2020); the reliability and validity studies themselves (Wilder et al., 2019; Cymbal et al., 2020) are not reproduced here and no further coefficients are claimed.
Who can use itSupervisors without behavior-analytic training have used the PDC–HS successfully (Bowe & Sellers, 2018; Smith & Wilder, 2018, as cited in Brand et al., 2022). Brand et al. recommend it for every concern a new supervisor meets, and for experienced supervisors as a periodic self-check, when a concern has not responded to the usual strategies, and whenever bias toward the staff member is possible.
SettingsThe published studies are from clinics. School use is reported second-hand: preschool paraprofessionals’ error correction (Bowe & Sellers, 2018), paraeducators’ treatment fidelity (Collier-Meek et al., 2021) and school staff tardiness (Merritt et al., 2019), all as cited in Brand et al. (2022), and Hogan, Knez and Kahng (2015) on training school staff to run behavior intervention plans. Some participants in the school studies did not respond to the indicated intervention and the procedures had to be modified. The tool has not been validated for schools in the sources behind this form.
Why verifyInformant report can be inaccurate or biased, which is why eight items are answered only after observation or a staff interview, and why the Setup sheet asks whether the concern is a consistent pattern, whether others see it, and what permanent products show (Brand et al., 2022).

How the Figures Are Calculated

NO countThe number of items in the domain answered NO. Only the 22 numbered items count; the training sub-items 1a–1d, the follow-up boxes and the supplementary PDC items never do.
AnsweredItems with a Yes or No answer. N/A answers are reported separately and left out of this figure; no source says whether N/A reduces the denominator, so both percentages below are shown.
% NO of answeredNO count divided by the items answered Yes or No, times 100.
% NO of all itemsNO count divided by every item in the domain, times 100: the figure Wilder et al. (2018) computed, where the domain with the highest mean score was deemed the most relevant domain.
RankDomains are ranked by NO count, ties broken by % NO of answered. The completed PDC–HS 1.1 examples rank by count; Wilder et al. (2018) picked by percentage. When the two disagree, the table shows it and the decision is the team’s, recorded under “Priority order and rationale.”
No cut-offNo source gives a threshold. Every NO is an opportunity for intervention; a single NO (the materials do not exist) can require intervention regardless of what other domains score (Brand et al., 2022), which is what the override box records.
Skip rulesSub-items 1a–1d appear only when item 1 is Yes. When item 2 of the resources domain is N/A (no materials required), items 3 to 5 are set to N/A and the interview proceeds to item 6, as the instrument instructs. When item 6 is Yes, item 7 is N/A.
ChecksThe Scoring sheet lists items left unanswered and asterisked items answered without a verification method. The scores are provisional while either list is non-empty.

What This Form Does Not Claim

That any domain score is clinically significant; that the PDC–HS is validated for schools or teachers; that the indicated intervention is better than every alternative; that running it changes student outcomes (no study measured the student); that the supplementary PDC items are part of the PDC–HS; or that supervisor self-administration or staff self-report has been validated. Those are open questions in the sources, and the form is written so that it cannot be read as answering them.
